# Own the Loop That Rivals Keep Splitting

> **Decision since this report:** GRIT launches globally. Where this report recommends prioritising West African food data or a Nigeria-first launch, read it as "regional depth where users' searches miss", with West Africa as one candidate among several. See `docs/PLAN_PHASES_5_TO_9.md`, which also splits this report's Phase 5 changes into Phases 5–9, and renumbers its Phases 6–8 as 10–12.

GRIT's durable moat is not any single feature. It is a **closed loop** in which what a person eats sets how they train, how they slept and recovered adjusts both, and a small group of friends holds them to it. Each turn of that loop leaves behind personal data (food memory, an expenditure history, a burn factor, coach memory, a social graph) that a rival cannot copy on day one. That is the only defensible position left. In 2026 every single-domain feature GRIT has or plans became a free or bundled platform feature. Apple announced adaptive strength plans on 3 November 2026. Strava shipped a set-by-set workout log. Garmin, Google and Samsung added food logging to their coaches, and WHOOP shipped the same editable memory and proactive check-ins GRIT already has. None of them connects structured nutrition to training and recovery across devices with a social layer. MacroFactor, the closest rival, sells nutrition and workouts as two apps with "no shared coaching features" ([MacroFactor](https://macrofactor.com/workouts/)). GRIT should therefore build, in order, five things. First, accuracy-first nutrition with the best West African food data anywhere. Second, a progression engine that reads the food log and sleep. Third, a nutrition-aware recovery view built from raw signals rather than another opaque score. Fourth, small competitive squads with forgiving streaks. Fifth, life-stage programmes (GLP-1, cycle and perimenopause) that only a cross-domain app can run. It should not build a video library, a GPS network, an opaque "readiness" or "biological age" score, fertility predictions, lab interpretation, or anything that sends health signals to ad tech. Phase 5 is the right foundation. It needs West African dish data, voice set logging, an expanded health import and a trust-and-safety baseline added. The race-prediction model should move out, and the data-consent decision should be split into separate opt-ins.

## Single-domain features became free in 2026, so the moat must be the loop

The year's product news reads like a list of GRIT's roadmap being given away. Google renamed Fitbit to Google Health and launched a Gemini coach covering photo and conversational meal logging, macros, water and calorie targets at **$9.99/month** ([TechCrunch mirror](https://hn.jakechampion.name/reader/https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/07/googles-9-99-per-month-ai-health-coach-launches-may-19/)). Samsung's Health Assistant beta ties sleep, activity, nutrition and vitals together with Scan Meal and voice logging, and Samsung Health has **no subscription** ([Tech-ish](https://tech-ish.com/2026/07/21/samsung-health-assistant-ai-chatbot-beta/)). Garmin added a food database, barcode and AI photo logging to Connect+ in January 2026 ([DC Rainmaker](https://www.dcrainmaker.com/2026/01/garmin-connect-nutrition-logging-connect.html)). WHOOP announced **My Memory and Proactive Check-Ins** on 8 May 2026, the pattern GRIT shipped in Phase 4 ([Business Wire](https://businesswire.com/news/home/20260508464188/en/WHOOP-Expands-Health-Platform-with-On-Demand-Clinician-Access-and-New-AI-Features)). Apple's Fitness+ "Gym Strength" builds four-week progressive plans that adapt from logged reps and load, across 350+ exercises, inside a $9.99/month subscription ([9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2026/11/03/apple-announces-fitness-gym-strength-program-with-personalized-workout-plans/)). Strava launched a Workout Log with sets, reps, weight and muscle maps in May ([Strava press](https://press.strava.com/articles/strava-overhauls-strength-experience-with-expanded-partner-ecosystem-new-workout-log-and-muscle-maps)).

The consequence for GRIT is blunt. An AI coach with memory, photo logging, basic set logging and simple weekly progression are now **table stakes**. They are worth building well, but they are not reasons to switch or stay. What every one of these products still lacks is in their own descriptions. Garmin's Training Readiness uses sleep, recovery time, load, HRV and stress, but **no nutrition** ([evo listing](https://evo.com/computers/garmin-forerunner-265-smartwatch)). WHOOP has **no native food logging**; it imports from MyFitnessPal, Lose It! and Cronometer through Apple Health ([WHOOP support](https://support.whoop.com/s/article/Apple-Health-Integration)). Hevy Trainer progresses with double progression and "does not use sleep/HRV/readiness" ([prpath.app](https://prpath.app/blog/hevy-app-review-2026.html)). Google publishes no accuracy data for its photo estimates. Each platform's coach mainly reasons over its own hardware, so a Samsung user cannot run a challenge with an iPhone-owning friend. Apple scaled back its paid AI coach in February 2026 ([9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2026/02/05/apple-reportedly-scales-back-plans-for-ai-powered-health-coach/)), which leaves the largest premium wearable base with no first-party cross-domain coach.

A durable moat for GRIT therefore comes from five compounding assets that grow with use and do not transfer.

| Moat asset | How it compounds | Who can copy it, and how fast |
|---|---|---|
| Per-user models: food memory, expenditure history, burn factor, coach memory | Each log and correction makes targets and logging more accurate for that person | Nobody, without the person's history; switching means starting over |
| Regional food data: West African dishes, local names, local units, a labelled meal-photo corpus | Every contribution and correction improves search and the matcher for everyone | Incumbents could, but have no commercial incentive; local startups are small |
| Cross-domain inference: nutrition × training × recovery × cycle × medication | Rules and later models learn from joint data that single-domain apps never see | Only an app holding all streams; MacroFactor is closest and has not linked them |
| Social graph: squads, buddies, crews | Each friend invited raises the cost of leaving | Strava and Hevy have graphs but no nutrition; platforms are OS- or hardware-locked |
| Trust position: no ad tech on health data, free basics, published accuracy | Reputation accrues while rivals tighten paywalls and face privacy suits | Hard for incumbents whose revenue depends on paywalls and ads |

The category's economics make this the right battlefield. Health and fitness converts trials well (median **37.7%** trial-to-paid) ([RevenueCat SOSA 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps)), but it has the **lowest first-renewal retention of any category at 30.3%** ([Adapty](https://adapty.io/state-of-in-app-subscriptions-2026)). Day-30 retention for installs sits at roughly 3–8% ([Business of Apps](https://businessofapps.com/data/health-fitness-app-benchmarks)). Conversion is not the problem; year two is. Compounding personal data and friends are the levers that attack year two, so GRIT's product strategy and business strategy point the same way.

## Nutrition: accuracy and the West African table beat more AI

GRIT already owns the right architecture: Smart log grounds items in the user's food memory, then a database, then a checked estimate. The research confirms the design and points to where it must go deeper. Photo-only AI still misses energy by about a third: Cal AI, Lose It! and MyFitnessPal photo features underestimated meals by roughly 33% in a 102-meal study ([Nutritional Outlook](https://www.nutritionaloutlook.com/view/study-finds-popular-ai-calorie-tracking-apps-underestimate-meal-energy-by-about-a-third)). Secondary summaries of a 2025 *Nutrients* study put ChatGPT-5 at about 30.5% error from the photo alone and about **13.9% when ingredients were supplied** ([Callie](https://www.mycallie.app/en/blog/ai-calorie-accuracy-research)); that second figure is unverified against the paper but consistent with everything else. On 15 standardised hospital meals the best AI models matched registered dietitians on energy and carbohydrate but were **significantly worse on protein and fat** ([PubMed 41901141](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41901141/)). That is where cooking oil, sauces and frying hide, and it is exactly what matters for jollof, egusi and fried plantain. The most strategically important finding is that GPT-4o **fine-tuned on 1,269 Japanese meal photos beat dietitians on all nutrients** ([PubMed 42410932](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42410932/)). Regional training data closes the accuracy gap. That makes a labelled West African corpus an asset rather than a nice-to-have.

The regional precedent is consistent. HealthifyMe built its database from Indian food data curated with India's National Institute of Nutrition and grew to 35M+ users ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HealthifyMe); [Inc42](https://inc42.com/buzz/healthify-raises-20-mn-for-us-expansion)). Fitia, with a per-country database and no unreviewed crowdsourcing, reports 10M+ users ([Fitia](https://fitia.app/es/ayuda/articulos/change-food-database-country)). West Africa has the open seed data, **WAFCT 2019 with 1,028 foods and 56 components** as a free FAO file ([FAO](https://openknowledge.fao.org/handle/20.500.14283/ca7779b)). The 2025 Nigeria Nutrient Conversion Table draws 72.1% of its items from it ([ISCIII repository](https://repisalud.isciii.es/entities/publication/2bd23db3-a295-4745-9d93-002b9f86c13e)). There is no scaled consumer app. The academic myfood24 West Africa project names **dish-level recipes** as the hard problem ([Proceedings of the Nutrition Society](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/proceedings-of-the-nutrition-society/article/assessing-diet-more-accurately-in-nigeria-development-of-myfood24-west-africa/D9D1209BA782B7DAEAC88D515F8AAB60)). Local calculators already describe portions in local units ("one wrap of eba = clenched fist") ([calculators-ng](https://calculators-ng.pages.dev/health/nigerian-food-calorie-calculator)). Raw ingredient rows are not the asset. The asset is recipes for composite dishes, names in Yoruba, Igbo, Hausa and Pidgin, swallow and soup portions in wraps, mudu, cups and fists, and a validation study that proves the numbers.

Two further nutrition moves address the main reasons people quit. Users drop weight-loss apps when they **reach their goal** and from **the stress of constant tracking** ([eMarketer](https://www.emarketer.com/content/ai-will-power-next-phase-of-health-app-growth)). A UCL and Loughborough analysis of 58,881 posts found the dominant negative themes were **shame at logging "unhealthy" food**, nagging notifications and unreachable algorithm-set targets ([EurekAlert](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/1102616)). Weight-related self-monitoring apps were associated with increases in disordered weight-control behaviours over time among emerging adults ([Journal of Eating Disorders via DOAJ](https://doaj.org/article/4d8f9f868a5641159d0b9b4e60926823)). A four-week intuitive-eating app improved diet quality **without increasing disordered eating**, though the study was small and short ([JMIR Human Factors](https://humanfactors.jmir.org/2026/1/e81439/PDF)). Oura's Meals deliberately skips calories and reports protein, fibre and processing level ([Android Central](https://www.androidcentral.com/wearables/oura-ring/oura-can-now-track-meals-and-glucose-parameters-for-us-users)). A maintenance mode after the goal, kinder copy, a hide-numbers mode and an eating-disorder off-ramp are therefore retention features and safety features at once.

| Rank | Nutrition feature | Status vs plan | Why it is defensible |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Accuracy you can see.** Database-grounded logging, a confidence badge per item, a one-question cooking-fat and oil clarifier, and a published validation of GRIT's estimates | Smart log built; 5B and 5D extend it | AI errors cluster in fat and protein; no platform publishes accuracy; credibility compounds |
| 2 | **The best West African food layer.** WAFCT and Nigeria NCT, dish-level recipes, local-language names, local portion units, label-photo contributions, a labelled meal corpus | 5A imports FAO table; dish, name, unit and corpus work is missing | Regional fine-tuning beats dietitians; incumbents have no incentive to build it |
| 3 | **Memory-first logging.** "My usual", meal templates, and per-person portion calibration | Built; calibration is outside Phase 5 | Compounds per user, cuts logging burden and LLM cost |
| 4 | **Kind adaptive targets.** Cycle-aware weight trend, maintenance-after-goal mode, calorie floors and loss-rate caps | Core built in Phase 4; additions are new | Fixes the "reached goal, left" churn and the water-weight misreads |
| 5 | **Safety and non-diet mode.** Hide numbers, a quality view (protein, fibre, plants, processing), ED screen with soft off-ramp | New | Research and UK law both push here; reaches the intuitive-eating segment |
| 6 | **Micronutrients with honest estimates** | 5C and 5D | Cronometer's niche, made free-adjacent; feeds BP, GLP-1 and lab features later |
| 7 | **Meal plans and grocery lists from the user's own foods** | New, as a coach action | Planners are commoditised at $40–60/yr and sit in separate apps ([ProMealPlan](https://www.promealplan.com/en/blog/eat-this-much-review-2026)) |

The earlier Phase 4 research deprioritised meal planning and restaurant data, and that still holds: meal plans belong as a coach action that writes into the diary, and restaurants stay folded into Smart log ("Chipotle bowl, chicken, rice") until a licensing decision is made.

## Fitness: a progression engine that reads the food log

Logging is commoditised. Hevy's Pro tier costs **$23.99/year** and its free logger, feed and PRs drove it to a self-reported 10M+ users ([SensAI, competitor source](https://www.sensai.fit/blog/hevy-review-2026); [Similarweb](https://www.similarweb.com/ja/app/apple/1458862350)). Adaptive programming costs $10–16 a month at Fitbod, Alpha Progression and Gravl. Premium periodisation (JuggernautAI, RP Hypertrophy) costs about $35 a month ([Garage Gym Reviews](https://www.garagegymreviews.com/juggernautai-review); [MesoStrength, competitor source](https://mesostrength.com/blog/rp-hypertrophy-app-price)). With Apple and Strava now in the space, GRIT cannot win on "log sets plus a four-week template". It can win on the one thing none of them does: **adjusting training to the diet and recovery data GRIT already holds**.

The evidence makes this productisable rather than speculative. An energy deficit of about **500 kcal/day prevented lean-mass gains** from resistance training while strength gains stayed comparable ([Murphy & Koehler 2022](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/sms.14075)). Protein beyond about **1.62 g/kg/day** added no further fat-free mass ([Morton et al. 2018](http://breathe-edu-downloads.s3.amazonaws.com/Morton-2018.pdf)). Proximity to failure affects hypertrophy more than strength ([Robinson et al. 2024](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40279-024-02069-2)). Lifters under-predict reps in reserve by about one rep, worst for beginners ([Stronger by Science](https://www.strongerbyscience.com/reps-in-reserve/)). The April 2026 ACSM position stand, an umbrella review of 137 studies, concluded that **individualising to increase participation matters more than fine-tuning variables** ([Newswise](https://www.newswise.com/articles/acsm-unveils-landmark-2026-resistance-training-guidelines-first-update-in-17-years)). That is an evidence-based case for GRIT's accountability layer as a training feature, not just engagement. Together these define an engine that no pure training app can match. In a deficit above 500 kcal, switch the goal to "preserve muscle, keep strength" and celebrate strength holding. When protein is under target most days, say the hypertrophy block is underfed. After a short night, hold the load.

Competitors' complaints supply the design rules. MacroFactor Workouts users report odd jumps (load raised after a 0-RIR set; a rep target dropping from 10 to 5 at the same weight) ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6737156524)). Fitbod workouts feel "repetitive no matter how you change the settings", there is no injury filter, and it cannot answer "should I deload?" ([Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/www.fitbod.me)). RP Hypertrophy fits poorly when training days shift ([MesoStrength](https://mesostrength.com/blog/rp-hypertrophy-app-review)). The answer is a deterministic, auditable progression engine (Phase 5H.3's double progression, with RIR optional and calibrated) that caps load jumps by equipment increment, progresses by session sequence rather than weekday, keeps a movement's history when an exercise is swapped, and has the LLM coach explain each change. It should never call a rule "AI". GRIT's current free-text weekly plan is the gimmick tier; Phase 5F's enum-constrained exercise IDs are the fix.

Endurance follows the same logic. Strava's 2026 moves made it a poor foundation for a third party. Its API terms let apps show a user's Strava data **only to that user** and bar its use in AI models ([road.cc](https://road.cc/content/news/strava-users-cant-publicly-share-data-third-party-apps-311329)). Since June 2026 developers must also hold a paid subscription ([GIGAZINE](https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20260602-strava-ai-scraping/)). HealthKit and Health Connect expose routes, distance and heart rate with consent. On Android, reading another app's routes needs a manually granted permission and a per-route consent dialog ([Android Developers](https://developer.android.com/health-and-fitness/health-connect/features/exercise-routes)), so the UX needs a "tap to attach route" step. The open gap is **fuelling**: race and long-run carbohydrate planning lives in small single-purpose apps such as Fuelstate at $49.99/yr ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6763126317)), and no source showed Strava, Runna or Garmin doing it. Runna's February 2026 injury coverage, with physical therapists linking injuries to pace progression ([the5krunner](https://the5krunner.com/2026/02/21/runna-ai-marathon-training-injury/)), and Trustpilot complaints about no way to pause for illness ([Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/runna.com?page=2)) define the safety requirements: conservative ramp caps and an easy pause mode.

| Rank | Fitness feature | Status vs plan | Why it is defensible |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Nutrition- and recovery-aware progression engine** (deficit, protein and sleep gating; capped jumps; explained changes) | 5H.3 v1 has the core; extend the rules | Nobody links training load to intake; MacroFactor explicitly does not yet |
| 2 | **Fastest logger plus "say it" set logging** ("bench 80 for 8, 8, 7"), screenshot or whiteboard logging, free plate and warm-up calculators, offline-first, Watch | 5G covers one-tap repeats; voice and photo are new | Reuses Smart log; Strava has only announced screenshot-to-log ([Strava press](https://press.strava.com/articles/strava-continues-to-evolve-strength-experience-with-new-coach-designed-strength-series-via-native-recording)) |
| 3 | **Personal burn calibration** from adaptive expenditure vs logged exercise | 5H.1 v3 | Needs both intake and training; MacroFactor computes expenditure without wearables or training ([Arvo](https://arvo.guru/vs/macrofactor)) |
| 4 | **Endurance package**: route, pace and HR import from health stores, carb periodisation and gel timing, ramp caps, pause and illness mode | New; absorbs 5H.4 race predictions | Fuelling sits between nutrition apps and running apps |
| 5 | **Special-population programmes**: GLP-1 muscle protection, women 40+, minimum-effective-dose beginners | New | Fragmented, content-led market; GRIT already knows deficit and protein |
| 6 | **Honest strength standards**: OpenPowerlifting "competitive percentile" plus a GRIT community percentile later | 5H.2; community percentile is new | OpenPowerlifting is public domain; Strength Level forbids copying its tables ([Strength Level](https://strengthlevel.com/data-licensing)) |

## Health: recovery context built from raw signals, not another score

Composite readiness and recovery scores are proprietary blends with **no independent validation** found. The research's search for readiness-score validity returned nothing usable. The underlying signals are uneven. Against an ECG reference, nightly RMSSD error ranged from **5.96% for Oura Gen 4 to 16.32% for Polar** ([Dial et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12367097)). Apple Watch underestimated deep sleep by about 43 minutes in an Oura-funded study ([PMC11511193](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/pmc11511193)). Apple reports SDNN from spot checks, while Health Connect exposes RMSSD, and HealthKit only adds an RMSSD type in iOS 27 ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/hkquantitytypeidentifier/heartratevariabilityrmssd.md)). Scores also harm some users: an Oura-funded SLEEP 2026 abstract found more than 30% of sleep trackers reported orthosomnia symptoms ([SLEEP](https://academic.oup.com/sleep/article/49/Supplement_1/A246/8674102)). Apple's own Sleep Score uses only duration, bedtime consistency and awakenings and leaves out stage percentages ([Apple support](https://support.apple.com/en-ie/123002)).

The defensible design is a **Recovery Context card**. It shows sleep duration and regularity, overnight resting HR, HRV as a z-score against the user's own baseline and source, respiratory rate and temperature deviation, each with plain-language drivers drawn from the food and training logs. An example: "RHR +6 bpm and HRV −18% vs your baseline; you logged three drinks and ate two hours before bed." It should never compare HRV between people or platforms, never paywall the user's own imported data, and offer a trend-only mode. Health Connect already carries nearly every input, including RMSSD, sleep stages, respiratory rate, skin temperature, VO2max, menstruation, mindfulness sessions and a NutritionRecord with caffeine ([Android Developers](https://developer.android.com/health-and-fitness/health-connect/data-types)). GRIT needs no wearable partnerships for version one. One caution: the research did not retrieve the physiology literature linking alcohol, late meals and deficits to HRV and RHR, or short sleep to next-day intake. Those links must be sourced before the app states them as causes, and the card should say "often goes with" rather than "caused".

GLP-1 support is the largest health opportunity and the clearest cross-domain play. **12% of US adults currently take a GLP-1**, up from 6% in May 2024. Use is higher among women (15% vs 9%) and peaks at ages 50–64 (22%) ([KFF](https://www.kff.org/public-opinion/kff-health-tracking-poll-prescription-drug-costs-views-on-trump-administration-actions-and-glp-1-use/)). Lean mass makes up roughly **15–40% of weight lost** ([Frontiers 2025 review](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/clinical-diabetes-and-healthcare/articles/10.3389/fcdhc.2025.1720794/full)). About **60% of lost weight returns within a year of stopping** ([medRxiv meta-regression](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.06.09.25328726v1.full)), and real-world data show up to 65% of users stop within a year ([Lancet eClinicalMedicine](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/eclinm/article/PIIS2589-5370(26)00240-3/fulltext)). Shotsy does dose logging well but its nutrition is thin ([helloregimen](https://helloregimen.com/blog/best-glp1-tracker-apps-2026)). MyFitnessPal bolted on a medication log. Noom and WeightWatchers turned into prescribers. Nobody runs the whole journey. Medication logging alone is table stakes. GRIT's version links side effects to logged foods, makes **lean-mass protection a measured programme** (two to three strength sessions a week, a protein floor, a weight-loss-rate warning), and switches the adaptive engine into an **off-ramp maintenance mode** as appetite returns. That last piece is the largest unmet need, and only an app with adaptive targets and strength logging can deliver it.

Women's health needs honesty more than features. Umbrella-level evidence finds **no meaningful influence of cycle phase on strength or hypertrophy** ([Colenso-Semple et al. 2023](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/sports-and-active-living/articles/10.3389/fspor.2023.1054542/full)), and performance is at most "trivially reduced" in the early follicular phase ([McNulty et al. 2020](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7497427/)). "Cycle-synced" workout plans are mostly marketing. What is supported is small luteal-phase rises in intake and expenditure ([International Journal of Obesity](https://www.nature.com/articles/0803699)), including a 6.9% rise in sleeping energy expenditure in one small study ([PMC6981303](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6981303/)), plus pre-menstrual water retention. The evidence conflicts: a 2025 preprint found intake steady across phases ([medRxiv](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.02.28.25323098.full.pdf)). So the feature should be modest. A **cycle-aware weight-trend filter** stops the adaptive engine, and Phase 5's burn calibration, from reading water as fat. The coach can say "a little more hunger is common this week" without promising phase-specific workouts. Perimenopause is the growth segment: Midi Health reached a $1B valuation ([Fierce Healthcare](https://www.fiercehealthcare.com/health-tech/womens-health-clinic-midi-health-closes-100m-series-d-round-hitting-1b-valuation)). A "women 40+" track (lifting for bone and muscle, protein, sleep, a symptom log feeding the coach) sits in the gap between clinics and symptom trackers.

The remaining health features rank below these because the evidence is weaker or the regulatory risk is higher. Mental-health apps produce small but reliable effects (g≈0.26–0.28), and they are larger when apps use **mood monitoring and CBT** ([World Psychiatry meta-analysis](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10785982)). A ten-second mood and energy check-in, breathing sessions written to the health stores, and CBT-informed coach prompts are therefore cheap and justified. For blood pressure, Apple's FDA-cleared hypertension notification catches only **41% of cases** ([AHA Hypertension](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/HYPERTENSIONAHA.125.26031)), while salt substitutes cut systolic pressure by about 4.6 mmHg ([TCTMD](https://www.tctmd.com/news/massive-ssass-study-shows-switch-salt-substitute-cuts-stroke-cvd)). GRIT's legitimate role is importing cuff readings, tracking sodium and potassium from Phase 5C, and running DASH-style habit challenges. The case for CGMs in non-diabetics "remains unclear" across 23 studies ([Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s40001-026-03920-0)), and over 15% of young healthy adults spike above 180 mg/dL ([PMC11822776](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11822776/)). Zoe dropped its CGM in 2025 ([The Grocer](https://www.thegrocer.co.uk/news/nutrition-brand-zoe-launches-radically-redesigned-new-app/709452.article)). CGM therefore fits as an occasional two-week "meal response experiment" read from HealthKit or Health Connect, not a subscription. For longevity, cardiorespiratory fitness is the best-supported trackable signal ([Mandsager et al. via TCTMD](https://www.tctmd.com/news/big-fitness-equals-big-survival-benefit-new-study-shows)), so a "fitness age" built on published VO2max percentiles is defensible and a proprietary "biological age" is not. Lab import comes last. Function is suing Superpower over how biomarkers are counted ([FITT Insider](https://insider.fitt.co/function-sues-superpower-over-biomarker-claims/)), and Health Connect's medical-records API is still experimental ([Android Developers](https://developer.android.com/health-and-fitness/health-connect/medical-records)). GRIT's angle is not selling tests. It is trending a few diet-relevant markers the user already has next to what they ate.

| Rank | Health feature | Regulatory risk | Why this rank |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Nutrition-aware Recovery Context card** (raw signals vs personal baseline, trend-only mode) | Low | Wearables lack food data; food apps lack recovery; GRIT has both |
| 2 | **GLP-1 companion**: journal, side effects vs foods, lean-mass programme, off-ramp maintenance, private groups | Low if no dosing advice | Large, growing, female-skewed market; nobody owns the full journey |
| 3 | **Cycle-aware targets** and a **women 40+ track** | Low (logging) | Fixes adaptive-target errors; avoids the cycle-syncing overclaim |
| 4 | **Mood and energy check-in, breathing, CBT-informed prompts** | Low if not therapy | Small, reliable evidence; cheap; feeds the coach |
| 5 | **Blood pressure**: import, sodium and potassium, DASH challenges | Medium | Strong diet evidence; must never classify readings |
| 6 | **CGM meal-response experiments** | Medium | Weak outcome evidence; suits prediabetic and GLP-1 users, not the young and healthy |
| 7 | **VO2max fitness age and Zone 2 minutes** | Low | Best longevity evidence; transparent formula |
| 8 | **Lab import, trend-only** | Medium–high | Closest to clinical decision support; needs counsel first |

## Social: small squads, fair matchups and forgiving streaks

GRIT's social layer is its hardest-to-copy asset, but the evidence says the wrong mechanics backfire. In Penn's STEP UP trial (602 adults, 24 weeks), every social arm raised steps while it ran. **Only competition in clusters of three stayed significant afterwards (+569 steps/day)** ([Penn Medicine](https://pennmedicine.org/news/news-releases/2019/september/using-a-wearable-device-to-exercise-more-add-competition-to-improve-your-results)). The Köhler effect shows people persist longer when the team result depends on the weakest member, but less when a partner is much better ([Kent KAR](https://kar.kent.ac.uk/41384)). Interviewed underperformers withdrew or hid the app when comparison felt unfair ([arXiv 2508.12388](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.12388)). Gamification's effect shrinks from g=0.42 to **g=0.15 about 14 weeks after it ends** ([JMIR](https://www.jmir.org/2022/1/E26779/)). Streaks motivate, but **breaking one demotivates and drives people away** ([Psychology Today summary of Silverman & Barasch](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/blog/ulterior-motives/202306/how-broken-streaks-sap-motivation)). Financial incentives add about 600 steps a day while they run, with contested carryover ([BJSM](https://bjsm.bmj.com/content/54/21/1259)). Self-funded deposits suffer **low uptake** ([PubMed 36201384](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36201384/)).

The design that follows is specific. Default to squads of three to eight with **matched-ability brackets or improvement-versus-own-baseline scoring**, so low performers can win. Add a weakest-link team goal with gentle nudges. Make streaks weekly ("three workouts this week") with freezes, rest days and make-up repairs, plus a coach "fresh start" message after a break. Give buddies a coach-written weekly digest, the cheap version of STEP UP's effective support arm. Rotate seasons and re-form squads every four to six weeks to fight decay. Compete on **behaviours, not biometrics**: protein days, a sodium budget, bedtime consistency, Zone 2 minutes, alcohol-free days. Device HRV error varies too much for fair ranking.

Above squads sits a monetisable format. Strava's clubs nearly quadrupled to 1M in 2025 ([SGB Online](https://sgbonline.com/stravas-year-in-sport-trend-report-highlights-gen-zs-passion-for-run/)). Ladder's coach-led teams charge **$29.99/month** and claim 300,000+ paid members, a company figure relayed by a competitor ([SensAI](https://www.sensai.fit/blog/ladder-app-review-2026)). Future cancelled its free AI coach in June 2026 to "double down" on human coaches ([Athletech News](https://athletechnews.com/age-of-ai-human-personal-trainers-might-become-a-luxury-future-caliber/)). That points to "**Crews**": groups of 5–30 led by a creator or coach, where the AI does the daily personalised work and the human sets the programme and shows up weekly, with GRIT taking a revenue share. Demand evidence for creator-led groups inside consumer apps is thin, so Crews should launch as a pilot with a handful of coaches. Shareable cards are a cheap growth lever: Hevy reported a 42% lift in shares from Stories sharing ([Meta case study](https://developers.facebook.com/success-stories/hevy/)). These cards must carry only what the user chooses, never auto-posted weight, sleep or medication.

| Rank | Social feature | Evidence basis |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Squads of 3–8, competition with fair brackets or improvement scoring | STEP UP; comparison-backfire studies |
| 2 | Forgiving weekly streaks with freezes and repairs | Broken-streak demotivation |
| 3 | Weakest-link team goals | Köhler and discrepancy effects |
| 4 | Coach-written buddy digest | STEP UP support arm |
| 5 | Behaviour challenges across food, sleep consistency and training | Device-fairness data; GRIT's unique metrics |
| 6 | Seasons every 4–6 weeks | Gamification decay |
| 7 | Creator- and coach-led Crews with revenue share | Ladder pricing; Future's retreat to humans |
| 8 | Shareable cards (opt-in content only) | Hevy Stories lift |
| 9 | Charity-forfeit commitment stakes (last, after legal review) | Low deposit uptake; gambling-law grey zone |

## Cross-domain integration is the part nobody can copy quickly

Everything above earns its moat status only if the domains talk to each other through one shared model of the person. Architecturally, that means one context store holding intake, targets, training, sleep and recovery, cycle, medication, mood and social commitments. Deterministic engines (adaptive targets, progression, burn calibration, streak logic) make the decisions, and the LLM coach explains, answers and proposes changes that the engines validate. This also contains liability. An auditable rule can be tested, capped and explained, whereas a free-text plan cannot.

The specific loops are where GRIT becomes uncopyable. In a deficit above 500 kcal/day, training switches to "preserve" mode and the coach reframes success. Low protein gates hypertrophy progress. After a short night, the progression engine holds load and the coach suggests a protein-forward breakfast. When a large, sustained deficit coincides with falling HRV and rising RHR, the coach proposes a diet break or deload. Long-run and race days trigger carbohydrate periodisation and a carb-load plan. GLP-1 status changes targets, coaching language and training emphasis at once. Cycle phase discounts water-weight noise in both the expenditure model and the burn factor. A recomposition dashboard (weight trend, protein adherence, strength trend, weekly volume per muscle, with the coach interpreting it) shows the loop on one screen. GRIT should also **write its nutrition, mindfulness and strength sessions back** to HealthKit and Health Connect. Health Connect's NutritionRecord already carries full macros and caffeine. Write-back makes GRIT the food source that WHOOP, Oura and Garmin users' apps read, which turns competitors' hardware into GRIT's distribution.

| Rank | Cross-domain loop | Domains joined | Who else does it |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Shared context model; engines decide, LLM explains | All | Nobody at scale |
| 2 | Deficit- and protein-gated training goals | Nutrition × strength | Nobody found; MacroFactor has no shared coaching |
| 3 | Sleep, alcohol and late meals → next-day load and food coaching | Recovery × nutrition × training | Nobody found; Garmin readiness ignores food |
| 4 | Personal burn factor for earnback | Nutrition × training | Nobody found |
| 5 | GLP-1 lean-mass protection and off-ramp | Medication × nutrition × strength | Partial (Noom claims, unverified) |
| 6 | Cycle-aware expenditure and burn calibration | Cycle × nutrition | None confirmed (needs a product check of MacroFactor) |
| 7 | Endurance fuelling and carb periodisation | Running × nutrition | Single-purpose apps only |
| 8 | Write-back to health stores | GRIT × every wearable | Standard for loggers, rare for AI-logged food |

## What GRIT should refuse to build

The discipline of saying no matters as much as the roadmap, because each item below either burns scarce build time on commoditised ground or creates legal or trust exposure that would undo the moat. GRIT should not build a **video class library**. Apple Fitness+ costs $9.99, and Peloton's shrinking base shows content alone does not retain. It should not compete with Strava on **GPS, segments and routes**, or build any feed or AI feature on **Strava API data**, which the reported terms forbid. It should not invent an **opaque readiness, healthspan or "biological age" score**. WHOOP Age is criticised for publishing no formula or confidence intervals ([Medium](https://medium.com/@cailbhe/is-whoop-really-able-to-measure-your-healthspan-728b88e69175)), and no composite score has independent validation. It should not **rank users on HRV, sleep stages or weight lost**. It should not sell **cycle-synced training plans** or predict **fertile windows**; Natural Cycles needed a De Novo classification to do the latter ([BioSpace](https://www.biospace.com/fda-releases-final-classification-for-natural-cycles-the-first-and-only-birth-control-app-in-the-u-s)).

It should not **interpret labs, blood pressure or glucose against clinical thresholds**, say "deficient", give **GLP-1 dosing or titration advice**, or take affiliate money from compounded-GLP-1 telehealth firms. It should not offer **pooled cash wagering** on goals. It should not let **under-18s** set weight-loss goals or see weight-loss leaderboards. It should not put **ad, attribution or analytics SDKs** on any screen that shows health data. It should not **paywall barcode scanning, core logging, raw imported data or the social layer**. It should not market a rule as "AI", or let the LLM emit free-text plans. It should not ship a second app for workouts, as MacroFactor did, or force a big-bang redesign with no revert path, as MyFitnessPal did. It should not build **camera form scoring for barbell lifts**: Peloton discontinued its Guide camera in 2025 ([The Clip Out](https://theclipout.com/peloton-guide-discontinued/)), and no product reliably judges heavy squats or deadlifts. A bodyweight rep counter for beginners is the narrow exception, later. Finally, it should not sell blood tests or hardware; importing what the user already has is cheaper and safer.

## Regulation draws bright lines at diagnosis, dosing and ad tech

The US rules loosened in January 2026 but kept the same bright line. The FDA's revised General Wellness guidance lets non-invasive estimates, including blood pressure, count as wellness if the intended use is strictly wellness. A "see a professional" nudge must not name a disease, call a value abnormal, use clinical thresholds or recommend treatment ([Covington](https://www.cov.com/en/news-and-insights/insights/2026/01/fda-issues-revised-guidance-on-general-wellness-products)). Invasive sensing such as CGMs is outside the policy, but firms read the guidance's example as allowing AI nutrition feedback on data from a separately cleared CGM ([Emergo by UL](https://www.emergobyul.com/news/fda-updates-broaden-wellness-and-clinical-decision-support-software-devices-under-enforcement)). WHOOP's BP feature drew a warning letter in July 2025, and FDA closed it in June 2026 after labelling changes. That was **non-enforcement, not a safe harbour** ([MedTech Dive](https://medtechdive.com/news/fda-drops-whoop-warning-letter-over-blood-pressure-feature/823652)). Signos needed FDA clearance for a CGM-based weight-management claim ([Fierce Biotech](https://www.fiercebiotech.com/medtech/fda-clears-signos-over-counter-cgm-powered-weight-loss-app)). FDA has also sent dozens of warning letters to GLP-1 telehealth firms over compounding claims ([BioSpace](https://www.biospace.com/fda/fda-sends-30-warning-letters-to-continue-clampdown-on-compounded-glp-1s)).

Privacy is the bigger practical risk. GRIT is almost certainly not a HIPAA entity. It falls under FTC Section 5, the Health Breach Notification Rule and state consumer-health laws. The 2024 HBNR treats unauthorised sharing, such as to ad platforms, as a breach ([National Law Review](https://natlawreview.com/article/ftc-reverses-course-health-app-privacy)). The FTC's September 2026 rescission of its 2021 policy statement has left commentators split on scope ([AHA News](https://www.aha.org/news/headline/2026-09-10-ftc-rescinds-policy-statement-extending-breach-notification-rule-apps-devices-collect-health)). GoodRx paid $1.5M and was banned from sharing health data for advertising ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2023/02/ftc-enforcement-action-bar-goodrx-sharing-consumers-sensitive-health-info-advertising)). Washington's My Health My Data Act has a private right of action, and its first class action targets an ad SDK ([Orrick](https://www.orrick.com/en/Insights/2025/02/First-Lawsuit-Filed-Under-Washingtons-My-Health-My-Data-Act)). Apple bars using HealthKit data for advertising and storing it in iCloud ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)). Since November 2025, Apple's guideline 5.1.2(i) also requires disclosure and **explicit permission before sharing personal data with third-party AI** ([Apple Developer News](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=ey6d8onl)), which covers GRIT's Azure OpenAI calls. Azure does not give prompts to OpenAI or use them for training. Default abuse monitoring can retain flagged samples, though, and Global deployments can process data in any region ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/foundry/responsible-ai/openai/data-privacy)). In the EU, the AI Act's chatbot disclosure duty has applied since **2 August 2026** ([CSA](https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/research/csa-research-note-eu-ai-act-article-50-transparency-20260729/)). EDPB Opinion 28/2024 explicitly does not extend legitimate interest to special-category health data ([Hunton](https://huntonak.com/privacy-and-information-security-law/edpb-publishes-opinion-on-processing-of-personal-data-in-the-context-of-ai-models)), so model training on health data needs explicit opt-in. Illinois, Nevada, Utah and California restrict AI that offers therapy or implies licensure ([Verticomply](https://verticomply.com/blog/state-ai-healthcare-laws-2026)). The UK Online Safety Act treats eating-disorder content as "primary priority content" that services with user-to-user features must keep from children ([Pinsent Masons](https://www.pinsentmasons.com/en-gb/out-law/news/uk-childrens-safety-codes-published)). That brings GRIT's feed into scope. Texas's App Store Accountability Act is enforceable after the Supreme Court declined to block it ([Lexology](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=60cfedda-77a9-4539-b439-a68c4f42b52a)). Nigeria's GAID, effective 19 September 2025, adds DPIAs for health and profiling, registration for major-importance controllers and 72-hour breach notice ([DLA Piper](https://privacymatters.dlapiper.com/2025/06/nigeria-ndpc-issues-gaid-key-compliance-insights/); [Lexluminar](https://lexluminar.com/navigating-the-ndpcs-general-application-and-implementation-directive-gaid-2025/)).

| Area | Do | Don't |
|---|---|---|
| Recovery, HRV, sleep | "Your resting HR is above your 30-day baseline"; readiness and recovery language | Illness, infection, arrhythmia or "you may be getting sick" claims |
| Micronutrients | "You logged less iron than the reference intake this week" (Phase 5C wording is right) | "You are iron deficient"; supplement doses to treat |
| Blood pressure | Display and trend cuff readings from health stores; sodium and DASH habits | Classify against hypertension stages; alerts; estimating BP from sensors |
| CGM | Show a cleared CGM's glucose next to meals; "larger rise than your usual breakfast" | Prediabetes, insulin-resistance or diabetes claims; CGM "weight-loss system" claims |
| Labs | Store and trend the user's own values; "discuss with your clinician" | Flag abnormal values vs reference ranges; treatment advice |
| Cycle | Period logging, water-weight filtering, symptom-aware coaching | Fertile-window, contraception or conception predictions; PCOS or perimenopause detection |
| GLP-1 | Dose and side-effect journal, reminders, protein and lifting coaching | Dose or titration advice; promoting or earning affiliate fees from compounded GLP-1s |
| AI coach | Label it "AI coach"; disclose AI (EU Art. 50); refuse diagnosis and dosing; route crisis and ED language to resources | "Nutritionist", "dietitian", "therapist" titles; mental-health treatment framing |
| Calorie targets | Floors and loss-rate caps set with a dietitian; 18+; ED screen | Targets for minors, low-BMI users or declared ED history |
| Privacy stack | Server-side analytics with health-free event names; separate consents; consumer-health privacy notice; Apple 5.1.2(i) consent naming Microsoft; Azure Data Zone, stateless APIs, Modified Abuse Monitoring request | Ad or attribution SDKs on health screens; custom audiences from health users; HealthKit data in iCloud or marketing |
| Model training | Separate, default-off opt-in for health data; de-identify; honour deletion | Bundling training consent into general terms; EU legitimate-interest reliance |
| Stakes | Charity forfeits or GRIT-funded prizes with no purchase needed | Pooled "losers pay winners" cash pots without gaming counsel |
| Stores | Google Play Health apps declaration listing every health feature; identical Play and Health Connect privacy policies ([Play Console Help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/14738291?hl=en)) | Requesting Health Connect permissions GRIT does not use |

Several points need counsel before launch rather than after. They include the exact text of the 2026 FDA guidance for the BP, CGM and lab features; WA MHMDA consent flows; the GDPR DPIA and EU and UK representatives; UK Online Safety Act scoping; Texas age signals; NDPC registration thresholds; and any stakes feature. The calorie floors usually quoted (about 1,200 kcal for women and 1,500 for men) are clinical heuristics, not law.

## Business model: free core, paid depth and a naira checkout

The benchmarks pull in two directions, and GRIT should resolve them deliberately. Hard paywalls convert about five times better than freemium by day 35 (10.7% vs 2.1%), with **the same year-one retention** ([BuildMVPFast summary of RevenueCat](https://www.buildmvpfast.com/blog/hard-paywall-vs-free-trial-revenuecat-indie-app-2026)). The steelman for a hard paywall is real. But GRIT's moat depends on a social graph, and a social app cannot hard-paywall the friend someone invites. MyFitnessPal's barcode paywall and Strava's string of paywall moves show that gating basics costs goodwill. The right split is a **generous free core**: logging, barcode, the regional database, basic adaptive targets, social, raw imported data and GLP-1 logging, which MyFitnessPal already made free ([GlobeNewswire](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/04/28/3282728/0/en/MyFitnessPal-Launches-Comprehensive-GLP-1-Support-Helping-Users-Stay-Consistent-and-Build-Habits-Alongside-Medication-to-Maximize-Their-Experience.html)). The paid tier is **depth**: AI logging beyond a monthly cap, the full coach, micronutrients, the nutrition-aware progression engine, the Recovery Context drivers, meal plans and the life-stage programmes. Trials of 17–32 days convert at a **42.5% median vs 25.5%** for trials under four days ([RevenueCat](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/free-trial-length)), so a two- to four-week trial fits.

Price sits in a crowded band. MyFitnessPal Premium costs $79.99/yr, Google Health Premium $99/yr, MacroFactor $71.99 per app or $89.99 for both, and Hevy Pro $23.99. A single GRIT plan near **$59.99/yr in the US and UK** would undercut every integrated rival while charging for more than Hevy. That figure is a judgment, not a tested price. AI apps earn about 41% more per payer but retain worse ([SaaStr](https://saastr.com/the-top-10-learnings-from-revenuecats-state-of-subscription-apps-how-115000-mobile-apps-deliver-16b-in-revenue-whats-working-whats-quietly-killing-growth)). That argues for capping free AI calls and leaning on food memory to cut inference cost. A **squad or family plan** fits the social core; the research found no competitor offering one. Monthly plans and strong win-back offers matter because annual subscribers rarely return after cancelling ([LAVX News](https://news.lavx.hu/article/annual-app-subscribers-almost-never-return-after-canceling-new-revenuecat-report-shows)). Peloton's churn rise, about half from mistimed payment-failure emails ([The Clip Out](https://theclipout.com/peloton-subscriber-churn-q4-fy26/)), makes dunning a cheap fix. Crews add a revenue-share line at Ladder-like prices. B2B2C comes later and is slower. Discovery Vitality pays **up to 75% back on healthy groceries** ([Discovery](https://www.discovery.co.za/vitality/healthy-food)), and employers are funding clinical nutrition and GLP-1 programmes ([Pulse](https://www.pulse.bot/healthtech/news/carrum-virta-join-forces-on-employer-weight-management-solution-e82ec845-c072-4a50-b8d5-280b83b1a4a8)). GRIT's natural B2B asset is consented, verified behaviour (logs, habits, challenge completion) for insurers, employers and Nigerian HMOs. Any US health-plan partnership would make GRIT a HIPAA business associate.

Nigeria and West Africa should be treated as **the data wedge first and a revenue market second**. Smartphone penetration reached **75% in 2025**, and about 88–90% of devices run Android ([BrandSpur on KPMG](https://brandspurng.com/2026/09/26/nigerias-smartphone-penetration-rises-to-75-in-2025-kpmg-report-finds/); [EdgeX](https://pro.edgex.exchange/en-US/news/article/android-powers-88-of-nigerian-smartphones)). Google Play accepts naira only through Verve cards ([Techpoint Africa](https://techpoint.africa/2023/07/26/nigerian-verve-purchase-apps-google-naira/)). Developers route around this with **Paystack web checkout synced through RevenueCat** ([DEV](https://dev.to/igbojionu/stop-leaving-money-on-the-table-how-i-bridged-paystack-revenuecat-for-my-mobile-app-a-nigerian-f56)). Whether that route is allowed for in-app digital subscriptions under current Play policy must be checked before launch. The local competitors are early: Zayvo is iOS-only ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6790803253)), WELFAS launched in Lagos in 2026 ([BusinessDay](https://businessday.ng/life/article/firm-launches-ai-powered-solution-to-tackle-lifestyle-diseases/)), and Platos Health raised a $1.4M pre-seed for a premium device ([TechCabal](https://techcabal.com/2025/05/07/platos-health-raises-pre-seed/)). The most dangerous substitute is Samsung's free assistant on Galaxy phones, if it rolls out internationally. Food affordability, not app price, is the binding constraint on healthy diets in Nigeria ([GAIN](https://nutritionnigeria.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/GAIN-Unerstanding-the-cost-of-a-healthy-diet.pdf)). Budget-aware suggestions built on local staples are therefore a feature, not a discount. The playbook is Android-first, naira pricing with weekly or monthly micro-plans, a low-data mode, a free tier generous enough to drive contributions, and NDPA compliance before launch. One inference is untested but strategically important: the same West African food layer serves the **Nigerian and Ghanaian diaspora in the UK and US**, where card payments are easy and ARPU is higher. The diaspora is a plausible first paying segment for the regional moat.

## Roadmap: finish Phase 5 with five additions, then close the loop

Phase 5 is the right foundation. It moves GRIT off live Open Food Facts calls, adds micronutrients, links plans to a real exercise library, and starts the nutrition-aware progression engine, the first true cross-domain loop. Apple's Gym Strength announcement changes its emphasis, not its direction: the set logger must be the fastest available and integrated, because a plain logger is now free. Five gaps need closing inside the phase. West African data needs to be dish-level. Health import needs to cover what the progression and burn models consume. Set logging needs Smart log's voice pattern. A trust-and-safety baseline is overdue because the social feed and AI coach are already live. And the consent decision needs splitting.

On the social side it ships **Squads v2**: brackets or improvement scoring, weakest-link goals, weekly streaks with freezes and repairs, the coach-written buddy digest, behaviour challenges across food, sleep consistency and training, and four-to-six-week seasons. It adds a ten-second **mood and energy check-in** with breathing sessions written to the health stores and crisis routing. It ships the **GLP-1 companion** (journal, side effects versus foods, lean-mass programme on the Phase 5 engine, off-ramp maintenance, private groups), the first life-stage programme because it mostly reuses Phase 4 and 5 parts. Opt-in shareable cards finish the phase. Workout write-back to the health stores belongs here too.

**Phase 7, "Reach and revenue" (about 10–12 weeks).** This phase builds markets and monetisation on the moat. It covers the **Nigeria and diaspora launch package**: Paystack and naira micro-plans after a Play-policy check, a low-data mode, Android-first polish, and budget-aware local meal suggestions. It adds **coach meal plans and grocery lists** from the user's own foods, the **non-diet and hide-numbers mode** with a quality view, **cycle-aware coaching and the women 40+ track**, the **endurance package** (health-store route import with privacy zones, fuelling and carb periodisation, ramp caps, pause and illness mode, race predictions from 5H.4), a pilot of **Crews** with a few coaches on revenue share, and the **squad or family plan**. Per-person portion calibration and a West African photo model fine-tuned on the contribution corpus belong here once the corpus is large enough.

**Phase 8, "Biomarkers and partners" (after counsel signs off).** This phase covers **blood pressure** import with sodium budgets and DASH challenges, **CGM meal-response experiments** read from the health stores, a **VO2max fitness age** with Zone 2 minutes, **trend-only lab import** (Apple clinical records plus PDF extraction), progression v2 learned from GRIT's own sessions, direct Oura or WHOOP integrations only if users ask for vendor scores, and the first **insurer, employer or HMO pilot** built on consented behaviour data. Charity-forfeit stakes, a beginner bodyweight rep counter and restaurant data stay in the backlog until the evidence or licensing case improves.

## Conclusion

The research changes the question GRIT should ask. It is no longer "which features do competitors lack?", because in 2026 the platforms filled most single-feature gaps for free. The question is which joins they cannot make. Wearable companies have sensors but no structured food data. Food apps have intake but no recovery or training model. Training apps have sets but no diet. Every platform is tied to one ecosystem. GRIT's rare position is that it already holds all four streams plus a social graph, so its best investments are the joins: the engine that holds a squat because the user is 700 kcal under and slept five hours; the off-ramp that keeps GLP-1 weight off; the expenditure model that knows the scale jump is water; the squad that competes fairly on protein days rather than HRV. Each join is a small build on Phase 5's data, and each makes the next one cheaper.

Two less obvious implications follow. First, restraint is part of the moat. In a category where rivals are paywalling basics, facing privacy verdicts and drawing FDA letters, refusing ad SDKs, refusing to diagnose and keeping basics free is a positioning that incumbents structurally cannot match. Second, the West African food layer is worth more than its market size suggests. It produces the labelled data that makes GRIT's AI measurably more accurate. It opens a diaspora segment that pays in pounds and dollars. And it proves the thesis that accuracy on your own food, rather than generic AI, is what keeps people logging into year two.
