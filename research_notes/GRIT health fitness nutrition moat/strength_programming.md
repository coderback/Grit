# Strength Training, Hypertrophy and Workout Programming Apps: Competitive Landscape for GRIT (as of 5 Nov 2026)

Method note: research done 5 Nov 2026 via web search. Direct page fetches were blocked for most vendor domains in this session (macrofactor.com, hevyapp.com, arxiv.org, sensai.fit, openpowerlifting.gitlab.io), so many facts come from search-result summaries of those pages. A large share of 2026 "review" content is published by competing apps (sensai.fit, alphaprogression.com, mesostrength.com, gymnoteplus.com, prpath.app, setgraph.app, trainwell.net, repreturn.com, getfitcraft.com); these are flagged as **[vendor/competitor source]**. Prices are USD unless noted, with the date the source checked them where known.

## 1. Who offers what, at what price, with what scale

### Takeaway
The market splits into (a) cheap, fast **loggers** with social layers (Hevy, Strong, Setgraph, Liftosaur), (b) **algorithmic programmers** at $10-16/mo (Fitbod, Alpha Progression, Gravl, MacroFactor Workouts, Boostcamp Pro, Hevy Trainer), (c) **premium periodisation apps** at ~$35/mo (JuggernautAI, RP Hypertrophy), (d) **human-coach-led** subscriptions at $30-200/mo (Ladder, Caliber, Future), and (e) **platform giants** moving in (Apple Fitness+ Gym Strength, announced 3 Nov 2026; Strava Workout Log, May 2026; Peloton Strength+). Logging is commoditised and near-free; the paid value is shifting to adaptive programming, and no player yet couples programming to nutrition and recovery in one loop.

### Inferences
- Price anchors GRIT will be compared against: logging ~$3/mo (Hevy), adaptive programming $10-16/mo, nutrition+training bundle ~$7.50/mo annualised (MacroFactor $89.99/yr), premium periodisation $35/mo, human coaching $30-200/mo.
- Apple (Nov 2026) and Strava (May/Oct 2026) entering strength logging/programming means basic "log sets + simple weekly progression" is becoming a free platform feature. GRIT's differentiation cannot be the logger or a 4-week template; it must be the cross-domain loop (food + sleep + training + social accountability).

## 2. Programming and progression: what has evidence, who gets it right, where users complain

### Takeaway
The evidence base supports: more weekly sets → more hypertrophy (with diminishing returns), frequency mattering mainly as a way to distribute volume, proximity to failure mattering for hypertrophy more than for strength, and RIR/RPE autoregulation being at least as good as percentage-based loading. The 2026 ACSM position stand's headline is that participation and adherence matter more than fine-tuning variables. Apps mostly implement double progression or RIR-based rules; user complaints centre on bad load jumps, repetitive or random exercise selection, rigidity around schedules, and opaque "AI".

### Cited Findings

**Evidence**
- ACSM position stand, April 2026 (Med Sci Sports Exerc 58(4):851-872, open access): an umbrella review of 137 studies, >30,000 participants; first update since 2009; main message is that individualising programmes to increase participation matters more than adhering to specific prescription variables, and the biggest gain is from no training to any training — [Newswise](https://www.newswise.com/articles/acsm-unveils-landmark-2026-resistance-training-guidelines-first-update-in-17-years); [ACSM Science Spotlight](https://acsm.org/science-spotlight-acsm-releases-new-position-stand-on-resistance-training/); [Physiopedia](https://members.physio-pedia.com/members-news/new-acsm-resistance-training-guidelines-what-physiotherapists-need-to-know/)
- Schoenfeld, Ogborn, Krieger 2017 (15 studies, 34 groups): weekly sets as a continuous variable had a significant effect on hypertrophy (P=0.002); more volume → more growth — [ResearchGate PDF](https://researchgate.net/profile/Brad-Schoenfeld/publication/305455324_Dose-response_relationship_between_weekly_resistance_training_volume_and_increases_in_muscle_mass_A_systematic_review_and_meta-analysis/links/59dc0269458515e9ab4527d6/Dose-response-relationship-between-weekly-resistance-training-volume-and-increases-in-muscle-mass-A-systematic-review-and-meta-analysis.pdf)
- Schoenfeld et al. 2019 frequency meta (25 studies): frequency does not meaningfully affect hypertrophy when volume is equated; higher frequency helps when volume isn't equated — [core.ac.uk](https://core.ac.uk/download/200371264.pdf)
- Pelland et al. 2024 preprint (SportRxiv): meta-regressions of weekly volume and frequency on hypertrophy and strength; separate dose-response curves; related per-session volume preprint (Remmert, Pelland et al.) found positive dose-response for per-session volume with 100% posterior probability of slope >0 — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/384628335_The_Resistance_Training_Dose-Response_Meta-Regressions_Exploring_the_Effects_of_Weekly_Volume_and_Frequency_on_Muscle_Hypertrophy_and_Strength_Gain); [SportRxiv](https://sportrxiv.org/index.php/server/preprint/view/537) (preprints, not peer reviewed)
- Robinson et al. 2024, Sports Med 54:2209-2231: proximity to failure (estimated RIR) meaningfully influences hypertrophy but not strength gain; RIR was estimated from study descriptions (limitation) — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40279-024-02069-2)
- RIR accuracy: Halperin et al. scoping review/meta-analysis — lifters under-predict remaining reps by ~0.95 reps on average with high variability; accuracy better close to failure (1 RIR vs 3 RIR), worse for beginners, high-rep sets, unfamiliar exercises — [Stronger by Science](https://www.strongerbyscience.com/reps-in-reserve/); [dev.to summary](https://dev.to/yonatan_naor_5642e43447ea/rpe-vs-rir-how-lifters-actually-calibrate-effort-evidence-review-476g) (secondary)
- Helms et al. 2018 (21 trained men): RPE-based vs percentage-based loading both gained strength and muscle; non-significant, "likely small" strength advantage for RPE — [dev.to summary](https://dev.to/yonatan_naor_5642e43447ea/rpe-vs-rir-how-lifters-actually-calibrate-effort-evidence-review-476g) (secondary; primary paper not fetched)
- RIR proposed as useful for prescribing in special populations — [ResearchGate review](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/385937472_Repetitions_in_Reserve_An_Emerging_Method_for_Strength_Exercise_Prescription_in_Special_Populations)

**App implementations**
- Hevy Trainer: double progression (add load only after top of rep range on all sets); no readiness inputs — [prpath.app](https://prpath.app/blog/hevy-app-review-2026.html)
- MacroFactor Workouts: RIR targets, planned deloads, automated progression — [Feastgood](https://feastgood.com/marofactor-workouts-review/)
- RP Hypertrophy: adjusts weights, reps and set counts from pump/soreness/workload feedback (volume-landmark / mesocycle approach) — [App Store reviews](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/rp-hypertrophy/id1555614554?see-all=reviews&platform=iphone)
- Apple Gym Strength: 4-week plans, next week adapts from logged reps/load — [MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/11/03/apple-fitness-plus-gym-strength/)
- Ladder: coach-written programs, no algorithmic adaptation — [Ladder llms.txt](https://www.joinladder.com/llms.txt)

**User complaints**
- MacroFactor Workouts: App Store reviews say the algorithm makes odd jumps (e.g., after 100 lb × 6 at 0 RIR it raised load to 105; another saw rep target drop 10→5 at same weight/RIR; "Algorithm seems off"); a blogger found occasional "nonsensical" weight/rep suggestions and over-aggressive jumps; RIR-dependent logic fails if users misjudge RIR — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6737156524); [Feastgood](https://feastgood.com/marofactor-workouts-review/) (anecdotal)
- Fitbod: workouts feel repetitive after months ("repetitive no matter how you change the settings"); can't add custom exercises outside the library; odd early load suggestions (e.g., 35 lb lateral raises); plans before the session and doesn't adapt set-by-set; no injury filter; can't answer "should I deload this week?"; autoregulation can blunt linear progression — [Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/www.fitbod.me); [Lasta](https://lasta.app/blog/fitbod-review/); [Autonomous.ai](https://www.autonomous.ai/ourblog/fitbod-app-review); [AIToolsBakery](https://aitoolsbakery.com/blog/fitbod-review/)
- Fitbod billing: PissedConsumer 1.7★ from 68 reviews, dominated by billing/cancellation/double-charge complaints; crash and Apple Watch sync complaints — [PissedConsumer](https://fitbod.pissedconsumer.com/review.html); [JustUseApp](https://justuseapp.com/en/app/1041517543/fitbod-workout-fitness-plans/reviews)
- RP Hypertrophy: a reviewer says the "algorithm" is a simple calculation on feedback; users want soreness logged once before the workout; can't create custom muscle groups; poor fit for shifting weekdays/exercise swaps — [Trustpilot](https://ch.trustpilot.com/review/renaissanceperiodization.com); [App Store reviews](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/rp-hypertrophy/id1555614554?see-all=reviews&platform=iphone); [MesoStrength](https://mesostrength.com/blog/rp-hypertrophy-app-review)
- JuggernautAI: strength/powerlifting-only scope — [Garage Gym Reviews](https://www.garagegymreviews.com/juggernautai-review)

**Nutrition interaction (evidence)**
- Murphy & Koehler 2022 (Scand J Med Sci Sports): energy deficit of ~500 kcal/day prevented lean-mass gains from resistance training, but strength gains were comparable in deficit vs balance; authors advise avoiding deficits >500 kcal/day when preserving lean mass — [Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/sms.14075)
- Morton et al. 2018 (BJSM; 49 studies, n=1,863): protein beyond ~1.62 g/kg/day gave no further fat-free-mass gain with resistance training; upper 95% CI ~2.2 g/kg/day — [Morton 2018 PDF](http://breathe-edu-downloads.s3.amazonaws.com/Morton-2018.pdf)

### Inferences
- A defensible GRIT progression engine: double progression as the default (simple, robust for beginners), RIR as an optional layer for intermediates (with calibration prompts since beginners mis-estimate by ~1 rep), weekly set-volume tracking per muscle against evidence ranges, and deloads triggered by performance stalls plus readiness signals.
- The Murphy & Koehler finding is directly productisable: when GRIT's adaptive calorie target puts a user in a >500 kcal/day deficit, the engine should switch goal from "add muscle" to "preserve muscle, keep strength", hold volume, not chase load PRs as success metric, and the coach should explain why. Strength holding while lean mass gains stall is expected, so "your strength is holding during the cut" is a valid win message.
- Protein adherence (≥1.6 g/kg) is a natural gating input: "you hit protein 2/7 days; your hypertrophy block is underfed" is something no pure training app can say.
- The ACSM 2026 adherence message supports GRIT's social/accountability strengths as a legitimately evidence-backed lever, not just engagement.
- Complaints point to concrete design rules: cap per-session load jumps (e.g., by equipment increment and % of last load), explain every change ("why this weight"), let users swap exercises while keeping the progression history of the movement pattern, handle shifting weekdays (sequence-based not calendar-based), and never claim "AI" for a rule.

### Gaps
- No primary-source figures fetched for the Helms 2018 trial, Refalo 2023 meta on failure, or any 2025 autoregulation network meta-analysis (mentioned only by a blog; unverified).
- ACSM 2026 numeric recommendations (sets/reps/load/frequency) were not retrieved.
- No source documented how any app handles missed sessions specifically; RP's weekday rigidity is the only sourced item.
- Mike Israetel / Jeff Nippard YouTube reviews of these apps were not retrieved.

## 3. Logging UX: what makes it fast, and common complaints

### Takeaway
Hevy and Strong set the bar: pre-filled previous values, two-tap set completion, auto rest timer, set-type toggles (warm-up, drop, failure), supersets, plate calculator, offline reliability, Watch logging. Strong is widely seen as slightly faster; Hevy as more approachable and social. Differentiators at the margin are per-set rest timers, plate/warm-up calculators, and offline robustness.

### Cited Findings
- Hevy's own comparison calls logging speed a tie: both have auto rest timer, "2 tap set logging", set-type toggles — [Hevy vs Strong](https://www.hevyapp.com/compare/strong/) [vendor source]
- Independent reviewers lean to Strong for raw speed; Hevy "nearly as good but slightly more navigable for newer users" — [RepReturn](https://repreturn.com/strong-app-vs-hevy/); [Gainframe](https://gainframe.app/blog/hevy-vs-strong/)
- Rest timer gap: Hevy uses a single adjustable default per exercise and can't pre-set different rest for warm-up vs working sets; Strong supports short warm-up and longer working-set timers — [RepReturn](https://repreturn.com/strong-app-vs-hevy/) (verify in app)
- Plate calculator: conflicting claims (Hevy says both have one, free in Hevy, paid in Strong; RepReturn says Hevy lacks it) — [Hevy vs Strong](https://www.hevyapp.com/compare/strong/); [RepReturn](https://repreturn.com/strong-app-review/)
- Both support supersets and warm-up set types; Strong offers custom set types and granular edit history; Hevy offers set types, RPE, supersets, notes — [Setgraph comparison [vendor]](https://setgraph.app/ai-blog/hevy-vs-strong); [GymNote+](https://www.gymnoteplus.com/blog/hevy-vs-strong)
- Strong's offline-first focus reportedly yields fewer in-gym logging complaints; Hevy's social features add complexity and occasional bugs (anecdotal) — [Setgraph comparison [vendor]](https://setgraph.app/articles/hevy-vs-strong-app-comparison-2026)
- Fitbod: crashes and Apple Watch sync complaints — [JustUseApp](https://justuseapp.com/en/app/1041517543/fitbod-workout-fitness-plans/reviews)
- Apple Watch native Workout app: no sets/reps/weight fields or rest timer for strength (pre-Gym Strength) — [GymNote+](https://www.gymnoteplus.com/blog/how-to-track-strength-workouts-on-apple-watch); Gym Strength adds watch logging of exercises, reps and load — [MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/11/03/apple-fitness-plus-gym-strength/)

### Inferences
- Minimum bar for GRIT's logger: previous-session values pre-filled, one tap to complete a set, per-set-type rest timers with lock-screen/Live Activity, supersets/circuits, warm-up ramp calculator, plate calculator (free), RPE/RIR optional field, offline-first with sync, Watch logging, Apple Health/Health Connect write. GRIT can leapfrog with its existing "Say/Snap/Type" pattern from meal logging: voice ("bench 80 for 8, 8, 7") and screenshot/photo-of-whiteboard logging, which Strava has only announced.
- Paywalling basics (templates, plate calc) is a recurring irritant; keep logging free.

### Gaps

## 4. AI coaching in strength apps: real vs gimmick, user reaction

### Takeaway
Most "AI" in strength apps is rules-based progression (Fitbod, Hevy Trainer, RP); vendors themselves are increasingly honest about this. Genuine value is adaptive load/volume adjustment from logged performance; chat-based LLM coaching is mostly an add-on (HevyGPT). Users and trainers find generic LLM plans motivating for novices but blind to fitness level and recovery. No app yet uses nutrition, sleep and training together.

### Cited Findings
- Hevy describes Trainer as an algorithm, not AI; reviewers disagree on labelling — [prpath.app](https://prpath.app/blog/hevy-app-review-2026.html); [Hevy announcement](https://www.hevyapp.com/announcing-hevy-trainer/)
- Fitbod's "AI" is rules-based recommendation and cannot answer contextual questions like whether to deload — [SensAI [vendor/competitor source]](https://www.sensai.fit/blog/fitbod-review-2026)
- RP app's algorithm criticised as a simple feedback calculation — [Trustpilot](https://ch.trustpilot.com/review/renaissanceperiodization.com)
- Ladder deliberately positions as coach-designed, using AI only behind the scenes for coaches — [Ladder llms.txt](https://www.joinladder.com/llms.txt)
- Apple's Gym Strength builds on its Workout Buddy AI coaching tech (one source; unconfirmed) — [ATFW](https://www.atfw.ca/blog-3/apple-gym-strength)
- Trainer review of a ChatGPT-made 3-week plan: good exercise choice but ignored fitness level; recovery for heavier/higher-volume work under-weighted — [AOL/Women's Health](https://www.aol.com/chatgpt-created-3-week-booty-140000780.html)
- Tom's Guide: ChatGPT beginner plan felt doable and motivating; more personal detail improved it — [Tom's Guide](https://www.tomsguide.com/ai/i-asked-chatgpt-to-build-a-beginner-workout-plan-and-it-actually-made-me-want-to-go-to-the-gym)
- A 2026 arXiv paper studied consistency of LLM-generated exercise prescriptions via repeated generation (findings not retrieved) — [arXiv 2604.11287](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.11287)
- MacroFactor has no shared nutrition↔training coaching at launch — [MacroFactor Workouts](https://macrofactor.com/workouts/)

### Inferences
- GRIT's current free-text AI weekly plan is exactly the "gimmick" tier. The credible architecture: deterministic progression engine (auditable, consistent) + LLM coach that explains, answers questions, and proposes changes the engine validates. LLM output should be structured (exercise IDs from the library, sets/reps/RIR) not free text.
- GRIT's coach-with-memory is a real edge if it can reference training, food and sleep together ("you slept 5h and are 700 kcal under; today keep the top set at RIR 3").

### Gaps
- No Reddit sentiment data on AI plans was retrievable; no quantitative user-satisfaction comparison of AI vs human-coached apps.
- arXiv consistency paper's results unknown.

## 5. Form checking and rep counting (camera and wearables)

### Takeaway
Camera form feedback works for blatant errors and simple movements in controlled setups but is unreliable for heavy barbell lifts; the flagship consumer hardware (Peloton Guide) was discontinued in 2025. Wrist rep counting is mixed: Garmin counts natively but unreliably on compounds, WHOOP doesn't count reps, Apple's native app didn't log sets/reps until Gym Strength (manual). Users value automatic logging more than "form scores"; accuracy failures erode trust.

### Cited Findings
- Tempo (Studio/Move): rep counting generally dependable but occasionally drops reps (tight spaces, push-ups); form feedback catches gross errors (rounded back on deadlift) but is generic/infrequent on accessories; weight tracking is weak, and accuracy drops with non-Tempo weights; ~$59/mo cited — [Garage Gym Reviews](https://www.garagegymreviews.com/tempo-studio-review); [BarBend](https://barbend.com/tempo-studio-home-gym-review/); [Tempo Move review](https://www.garagegymreviews.com/tempo-move-review); [Agent Finder](https://agent-finder.co/reviews/tempo)
- Kemtai/Onyx: only low-rigor source; claims they call out real errors but no app reliably handles barbell back squats or heavy deadlifts — [AIToolsBakery](https://aitoolsbakery.com/blog/ai-pose-correction-apps-for-home-workouts/) (low reliability)
- Peloton Guide ($495 at 2022 launch) sales ended ~30 Jul 2025; existing owners supported; camera tech moved into "Plus" Bike/Tread/Row machines with auto rep counting and light form guidance limited to compatible classes — [The Clip Out](https://theclipout.com/peloton-guide-discontinued/); [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/general/peloton-cross-training-bike-tread-row-plus-machines-form-checking-cameras-rotating-screens-ai-price-launch-date-110042677.html?src=rss); [Engadget 2022](https://www.engadget.com/peloton-guide-machine-learning-strength-training-152637209.html)
- Academic work: FormCoach (2025) and learnable-physics form recommendation (2023) papers exist — [arXiv FormCoach](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.07501); [arXiv 2310.07221](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.07221)
- WHOOP Strength Trainer: does not count reps; estimates muscular load from accelerometer/gyro after user sets up exercises; needs manual set start/stop — [Riven](https://riven.fit/blog/can-a-smartwatch-count-reps); [the5krunner 2023](https://the5krunner.com/2023/04/27/whoop-ground-breaking-strength-training-or-damp-squib/)
- Garmin: native auto rep counting; a May 2026 community roundup calls it unreliable on most compound lifts; Garmin's own docs tell users to edit reps after sets; an unsourced "85-90%" accuracy claim for Venu 4 — [the5krunner Jun 2026](https://the5krunner.com/2026/06/05/2-the-deep-dive-fix-files-week-ending-6-june-2026/); [Kensoforge (unverified)](https://kensoforge.com/insights/best-fitness-trackers-for-powerlifting-in-2026)
- Apple Watch: native app records only HR, calories, time for strength; claim of auto rep counting on Series 9/Ultra 2 is from a vendor blog and unconfirmed; third-party rep counters (Motra, Gymatic, Rep Up) exist — [Riven](https://riven.fit/blog/does-apple-watch-count-reps); [Cora Health (unverified)](https://www.corahealth.app/blog/apple-watch-strength-training-coach)

### Inferences
- For GRIT, camera form analysis is a poor near-term investment for gym barbell users (accuracy, phone placement, privacy). A narrower, defensible use: on-device pose (e.g., MediaPipe/MoveNet — not researched here) for bodyweight/home movements (squat depth, push-up reps) aimed at beginners and older adults, framed as "rep counter + depth check", not "form score". Async video upload for coach/AI review of one set is lower risk.
- Wearables: prefer importing HR/strain from Apple Health/Health Connect and logging sets manually fast, rather than chasing auto rep counts.

### Gaps
- No independent accuracy benchmarks (rep-count error rate, form-error recall) for any consumer product.
- No user-survey data on how much lifters value form feedback.
- Hevy Watch rep counting: no source found (believed manual logging only; unverified).
- MediaPipe/MoveNet accuracy for strength exercises was not researched.

## 6. Social features in lifting apps and the strength-nutrition link

### Takeaway
Hevy pioneered a free Strava-like lifting feed (follows, PR sharing) and that drove its growth; Strava itself added strength logging and muscle maps in 2026. On nutrition, MacroFactor (separate apps, shared body metrics only) and Ladder (nutrition inside subscription since Oct 2025) are the only notable combined offers, and neither adapts training to diet or vice versa. ## 7. Special populations

### Takeaway
Beginners, women 40+/menopause, older adults and GLP-1 users are served by fragmented, often content-led apps; GLP-1 muscle preservation is a fast-growing need with clinical guidance (resistance training 2-3x/week + 1.2-1.6 g/kg protein + slower loss) but no app found that operationalises it end-to-end.

### Cited Findings
- GLP-1: consensus playbook is resistance training (Cleveland Clinic: at least twice weekly), protein ~1.2-1.6 g/kg/day, slower weight loss; lean mass often cited as 20-40% of weight lost (unverified secondary figure) — [Cleveland Clinic](https://health.clevelandclinic.org/exercise-for-glp-1-use); [GetFitCraft [vendor]](https://getfitcraft.com/science/glp-1-muscle-loss-research)
- Several registered trials test exercise + GLP-1 therapy (12-week exercise/nutrition program; resistance-training status in GLP-1 patients; RT and musculoskeletal health registered report), results not yet published — [ClinicalTrials NCT07554417](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07554417); [NCT07702461](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07702461); [NCT07091500](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07091500); [medRxiv registered report](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.06.24.25330195.full.pdf)
- No app comparison evidence for GLP-1 users found — same search
- Women/menopause: Owning Your Menopause (live strength classes, 2025 review), Flipping 50 (~$549/yr), Pvolve (from $14.99/mo, menopause and pelvic-floor programming), Sweat "Menopause Strong" (18 weeks, 2-3 × 30-40 min strength sessions), Fortify (women 40+, $9.99/mo or $49.99/yr) — [Woman&Home](https://www.womanandhome.com/health-wellbeing/best-strength-training-apps/); [HerDailyFit](https://www.herdailyfit.com/workout-apps-for-women-over-50/); [Ellim](https://www.ellim.app/blog/fitness/best-workout-apps-for-women) (several lists are self-promotional)
- A 2022 meta-analysis: resistance training in middle-aged and older women had a moderate-to-large favourable effect on strength — cited via [HerDailyFit](https://www.herdailyfit.com/workout-apps-for-women-over-50/) (secondary)
- Beginners: JuggernautAI explicitly not for beginners; Fitbod praised as a beginner help; Apple Gym Strength targets dumbbell-minimum, 20-60 min sessions — [Garage Gym Reviews](https://www.garagegymreviews.com/juggernautai-review); [Trustpilot Fitbod](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/www.fitbod.me); [MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/11/03/apple-fitness-plus-gym-strength/)
- Home/minimal equipment: Tempo, Peloton, Sweat, Apple (dumbbell-minimum); RIR useful for special populations — [ResearchGate RIR review](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/385937472_Repetitions_in_Reserve_An_Emerging_Method_for_Strength_Exercise_Prescription_in_Special_Populations)
- MacroFactor shares period tracking data between nutrition and workouts — [MacroFactor Workouts](https://macrofactor.com/workouts/)

### Inferences
- A "GLP-1 / weight-loss muscle-protection mode" is a natural GRIT wedge: GRIT already knows deficit size and protein; add a 2-3x/week full-body minimum-effective program, protein floor alerts, strength-maintenance as primary KPI, and a weekly coach check-in. Avoid medical claims.
- Beginners: double progression, machine/dumbbell-first exercise selection, and the ACSM "any training beats none" message favour a "minimum effective dose" plan that adapts to missed sessions instead of failing streaks.

### Gaps
- No sourced data on app usage by older adults or sarcopenia-focused apps.
- No evidence base comparing app features for women specifically (menstrual-cycle-based programming evidence not researched).

## 8. Public data and resources, and their licences

### Takeaway
Free, permissively-licensed exercise data exists (free-exercise-db, Unlicense, 800+ exercises with images); wger is AGPL code with per-exercise CC licences; ExerciseDB is commercial (11,000+ exercises with media via RapidAPI). OpenPowerlifting data is public domain and usable for strength standards; Strength Level's standards explicitly forbid copying into another product without a licence; Symmetric Strength terms were not found.

### Cited Findings
- **free-exercise-db** (yuhonas): Unlicense (public domain); 800+ exercises as JSON (id, name, force, level, mechanic, equipment, primary/secondary muscles, instructions, category, images); some fields null; a few duplicate images — [GitHub](https://github.com/yuhonas/free-exercise-db) (fetched 5 Nov 2026)
- **wger**: code AGPL-3.0+; initial exercise/ingredient data historically CC-BY-SA 3.0; newer README says per-exercise Creative Commons licences — [PyPI wger 1.4](https://pypi.org/project/wger/1.4/); [GitHub mirror](https://github.com/bbkz/wger)
- **ExerciseDB v2**: 11,000+ exercises, 20,000+ images, 15,000+ videos; self-serve plans on RapidAPI, one-off V1 dataset purchase, custom plans on contact; prices not published in results; competitor claims free tier ~10 req/day (unverified); promotional pages cite 100 req/mo free and $24.99/1,000 req (unverified) — [ExerciseDB Notion](https://exercisedb.notion.site/Table-of-Contents-1a6983b728ca80b69e85c5c74133220e); [GitHub](https://github.com/exercisedb/exercisedb-api); [DEV (competitor)](https://dev.to/furkanuruk/i-built-a-free-exercisedb-alternative-1300-exercises-with-gif-animations-5868)
- **OpenPowerlifting**: "we release all of our data into the public domain, and all of our code under AGPLv3+"; attribution requested ("This page uses data from the OpenPowerlifting project") but not legally required; data in GitLab opl-data — [OpenPowerlifting FAQ](https://www.openpowerlifting.org/dist/openipf/faq); [Hugging Face mirror](https://huggingface.co/datasets/fjolsvin/open-powerlifting)
- **Strength Level**: "free to use on our websites, but not free to copy into another product"; even free/open-source apps need permission; attribution does not replace a licence — [Strength Level Data Licensing](https://strengthlevel.com/data-licensing)
- **Symmetric Strength**: own terms not found; FitnessVolt republishes a "Symmetric Strength dataset" under CC BY 4.0 (FitnessVolt's licence, not proven to be Symmetric Strength's) — [FitnessVolt](https://fitnessvolt.com/strength-standards/strength-data/)

### Inferences
- GRIT can safely ship with free-exercise-db (public domain) as the seed library and build its own media; avoid copying Strength Level tables; derive its own standards from OpenPowerlifting (with attribution) and from GRIT users' own logged data over time.
- OpenPowerlifting represents competitive powerlifters (a strong, self-selected population, SBD only, raw vs equipped); standards derived from it will overstate "average" for gym-goers. Use it for "competitive percentile" and build a separate "GRIT community percentile" from user data; label clearly.

### Gaps
- ExerciseDB current pricing unverified.
- OpenPowerlifting LICENSE-DATA file not directly fetched (GitLab blocked); Symmetric Strength licence unknown.
- MediaPipe/MoveNet licences not checked (believed Apache 2.0; unverified).
