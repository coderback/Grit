## 1. Running/endurance apps: offers, prices, 2025–2026 changes, complaints, and fuelling

### Takeaway
The running stack in 2026 is consolidating around two ecosystems. Strava, preparing an IPO, owns Runna and has added strength, routes and AI. Garmin owns TrainingPeaks and TrainHeroic (since July 2026) and runs Connect+, which now includes food logging. Both put features behind paywalls, and each paywall move has drawn backlash. Race-day and long-run fuelling is still served almost entirely by small single-purpose planner apps and brand calculators, not by the major running or nutrition apps. That gap fits an app that already does nutrition, workouts and coaching together.

### Inferences
- Strava's 2026 moves (API paywall, endpoint removal, profiles behind login, recap paywall, Runna beginner plans made paid) show a company tightening its hold and its monetisation before an IPO. Each move produced visible user or developer resentment. A challenger that is generous on the free social layer can position against "paywall fatigue."
- Runna's injury story shows that AI plans which progress pace too aggressively are a real liability. GRIT's coach should build in conservative ramp limits (e.g., capping week-over-week load increases), readiness inputs (sleep, soreness) and an easy "pause or illness" mode. Runna's users explicitly ask for that pause.
- Endurance fuelling sits between nutrition apps (MyFitnessPal-style, which ignore training) and running apps (which ignore food). GRIT already has food logging, adaptive targets and workout import. It could own three things: carb periodisation on long-run and race days, a pre-race carb-load plan, and in-run gel timing. No major competitor does this.
- Garmin's Connect+ nutrition shows wearable makers moving into food logging. GRIT's edge must be the social and accountability layer and a better AI coach, not logging alone.
- GRIT should not try to beat Strava on GPS, segments and routes. Importing those activities and adding social, nutrition and recovery context is the better path. The 2026 API terms make Strava a poor data source for that, so HealthKit and Health Connect are the better route (see section 2).

## 2. Can a third-party app get GPS routes, pace, distance and heart rate from HealthKit, Health Connect and the Strava API?

### Takeaway
Yes, through the OS health stores, with user consent. HealthKit exposes workout routes (as CLLocation series), distance and heart-rate samples. Health Connect exposes ExerciseRoute, but reading another app's routes needs a separate, manually granted permission and a per-route consent dialog in the background. Strava's API is a poor foundation for a social app in 2026. Data may be shown only to the user it belongs to (no showing friends' Strava runs in a feed). Use in AI models is prohibited (Nov 2024 terms). Since June 2026 the developer must hold a paid Strava subscription.

### Cited Findings
- **HealthKit:** route reads need `HKSeriesType.workoutRoute()` authorisation as well as `HKObjectType.workoutType()`. A developer forum thread confirms reads failed until both were requested. Strava's own Apple Watch import asks users to enable "Workouts & Routes" — [Apple Developer Forums](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/83855); [9to5Mac 2020](https://9to5mac.com/2020/02/12/strava-apple-watch-import-workouts/); [CloudsPress](https://www.cloudspress.com/how-to-share-apple-watch-workouts-to-strava/). Points are read with `HKWorkoutRouteQuery`, which returns CLLocation batches. Apple's page "Reading route data" exists, but its content did not render, so the details come from model knowledge **[UNVERIFIED against live docs]** — [Apple Developer: Reading route data](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/reading-route-data).
- An Apple Community thread notes that Apple Watch workouts store GPS in the workout and the route data, while some third-party apps store it only in route data. Strava's Apple import covers only workouts from Apple's Workout app within a 30-day window — [CloudsPress](https://www.cloudspress.com/how-to-share-apple-watch-workouts-to-strava/); [Apple Discussions](https://discussions.apple.com/thread/8632769?page=1).
- **Health Connect:**
  - `READ_EXERCISE_ROUTES` "can only be granted manually by a user in Health Connect settings or in the route request activity (ACTION_REQUEST_EXERCISE_ROUTE)", and app requests for it are ignored.
  - For routes written by other apps, Health Connect returns `ExerciseRouteResult.ConsentRequired` even with "Always allow" access, so the user grants a one-time read per route through a dialog.
  - Heart rate needs `READ_HEART_RATE`, and routes are tied to exercise sessions, which need an exercise permission.
  - Health Connect also has a Training Plans data type.
  - Sources: [Android Developers: Exercise routes](https://developer.android.com/health-and-fitness/health-connect/features/exercise-routes); [HealthPermissions reference](https://developer.android.com/reference/kotlin/android/health/connect/HealthPermissions); [Training plans](https://developer.android.com/health-and-fitness/health-connect/features/training-plans).
- **Strava API (Nov 2024 terms, per news coverage):**
  - Third-party apps may display a user's Strava activity data only to that user, which kills cross-user leaderboards and feeds built on Strava data.
  - Data from the API may not be used in AI models or similar applications.
  - Apps must keep a UI distinct from Strava's.
  - Coaching and feedback platforms remain permitted.
  - Sources: [road.cc](https://road.cc/content/news/strava-users-cant-publicly-share-data-third-party-apps-311329); [MalwareTips/CyberInsider](https://malwaretips.com/threads/strava-tightens-api-policies-to-bolster-user-privacy-and-security.133808). The primary agreement at strava.com/legal/api could not be fetched.
- **June 2026:** Standard-tier API developers must hold a Strava subscription ($11.99/mo or $79.99/yr), and some endpoints have been retired — [GIGAZINE](https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20260602-strava-ai-scraping/); [AI Chat Daily](https://www.aichatdaily.com/ai-business/strava-puts-api-behind-11-99-paywall-blaming).
- Strava itself pipes user data to Claude and Gemini through its own MCP connector, subscribers only. Strava keeps AI use of its data for itself and its chosen partners — [Strava Help Centre](https://support.strava.com/hc/en-us/articles/15401531-strava-mcp-connector).
- Garmin also tightened API brand-attribution rules in 2025, which triggered the Strava–Garmin fight. Garmin's direct developer API is an alternative source that requires attribution — [Marathon Handbook](https://marathonhandbook.com/strava-dismisses-lawsuit-against-garmin-after-uproar-from-customers/).

### Inferences
- **Recommended path:** import runs, rides and walks with route, distance, pace and heart rate from HealthKit and Health Connect. Most watches (Garmin, COROS, Apple, Samsung, Pixel) write to these stores. Garmin and COROS writing route data into HealthKit or Health Connect is assumed but not verified here.
- On Android, background route sync from other apps needs per-route consent. Plan UX for this, such as a "tap to attach route" prompt on the imported workout card.
- Do not build GRIT's social feed or AI coach on Strava API data. The display-to-owner-only rule and the AI ban (as reported) would make it non-compliant. If a Strava link is offered at all, limit it to the user's private view.
- With first-party data from HealthKit and Health Connect, GRIT can legitimately show a friend's run map and pace in its feed (with privacy zones) and feed it to its own AI coach. That is the thing Strava now blocks for third parties.

### Gaps
- The verbatim Strava API Agreement and the current HealthKit docs could not be fetched (hosts blocked or page not rendered). Confirm the exact AI clause and display rules before building.
- It was not verified whether Garmin Connect and COROS write GPS routes (not just workouts) into HealthKit and Health Connect.
- Garmin Connect Developer Program terms and fees were not researched.

## 3. Classes and guided workouts: should a nutrition-first app offer content?

### Takeaway
Video class libraries are expensive, commoditised ($10–$30/mo) and under pressure: Peloton's subscriber base shrank 8.8% in FY2026. Strava's own answer in Oct 2026 was coach-designed structured strength and running workouts with live tracking, not video. For GRIT, structured, AI-generated or partner-sourced workouts plus creator-led programmes are the logical play, not a production studio.

### Cited Findings

- Peloton FY2026: 2.553M connected-fitness subscribers (−8.8% YoY) and Q4 churn of 2.2%; guidance for continued decline — [The Clip Out](https://theclipout.com/peloton-subscriber-churn-q4-fy26/).

- Ladder (strength, team-based programming): $29.99/mo with a 7-day trial. Users join one of 17 coach-led teams with six-week progressive programmes. It raised $105M Series B plus $90M growth investment from General Catalyst, and said it was "on track to exceed 400,000 members by end of 2025" (company claim, July 2025) — [Fortune 2024](https://www.fortune.com/2024/12/10/strength-training-ladder-app-users-growth); [Business Wire Jul 2025](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250707361917/en); [HCM](https://www.healthclubmanagement.co.uk/health-club-management-news/Ladder-raises-US105-million-to-grow-its-strength-training-app/354576). Revenue estimates range from about $100k to $5–10M a month, a sign of unreliable third-party data — [AppSprint](https://appsprint.app/ladder-strength-training-plans-monthly-downloads); [AppGoblin](https://appgoblin.info/apps/1502936453).

### Inferences
- A nutrition-first app should not build a video library. Content cost is high and differentiation is low against Apple Fitness+ at $9.99. Better options:
  1. AI-generated structured workouts (GRIT already has coach-generated plans) with live set and rep logging.
  2. Licensed or creator programmes.
  3. Ladder-style "teams": one coach and a programme per team, plus community. This combines content with accountability, and Ladder's funding suggests it monetises at about $30/mo.
- Pairing workout programmes with matched nutrition (e.g., a strength block with protein targets, a marathon block with carb periodisation) is something no class provider does.

### Gaps
- Alo Moves pricing and YouTube creator economics were not found (search budget exhausted).
- No data was found on Apple Fitness+ subscriber numbers or class-content retention vs. non-content apps.
- Content production costs per class were not found.

## 4. Evidence on social accountability mechanics: what works, what backfires

### Takeaway
The best RCT evidence (Penn STEP UP, 2019) found that competition in small groups beat support and collaboration. It was the only arm with a significant effect after the intervention ended (+569 steps/day). All social arms worked while running. Gamification has a small-to-medium short-term effect (g≈0.42) that shrinks to small (g≈0.15) about 14 weeks after it ends. Financial incentives raise steps by about 600/day during the incentive, with a weak and contested carryover. Self-funded deposit contracts get low uptake. Streaks motivate but broken streaks demotivate. Upward comparison on leaderboards makes some low performers hide or quit. Small groups, fair matchups, forgiving streaks and opt-in stakes are the design implications.

### Cited Findings

**Competition vs support vs collaboration**
- STEP UP (Patel et al., JAMA Intern Med 2019): 602 adults with BMI ≥25, 24-week intervention.
  - During the intervention, versus control: competition +920 steps/day, support +689, collaboration +637.
  - In the 12-week follow-up, only competition stayed significant (+569/day). Support and collaboration were not significant.
  - Competition used clusters of three with weekly leaderboard emails. In the support arm, a sponsor got weekly updates.
  - Sources: [Penn Medicine](https://pennmedicine.org/news/news-releases/2019/september/using-a-wearable-device-to-exercise-more-add-competition-to-improve-your-results); [EurekAlert](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/901675).
- **Köhler effect:** exercising with a partner in a "conjunctive" setup (the team result depends on the weakest member) increased persistence over solo exercise and over side-by-side partners. The effect was weaker when the partner was much better ("discrepancy effect"). A software partner worked but less than a believed-human partner (2014, n=120) — [Feltz/MSU theses via d.lib.msu.edu](https://d.lib.msu.edu/etd/1998/FULL_TEXT/view); [Kent KAR](https://kar.kent.ac.uk/41384).

**Gamification**
- Mazeas et al. (JMIR 2022), meta-analysis of 16 RCTs with n=2,407: g=0.42 (95% CI 0.14–0.69) on physical activity. The long-term effect, about 14 weeks after the intervention, was g=0.15 (0.07–0.23). Against active non-gamified controls, g=0.23. The authors describe the effect as "not only a novelty effect" but "volatile" — [JMIR](https://www.jmir.org/2022/1/E26779/).

**Financial incentives and commitment**
- Mitchell et al. (BJSM 2020), 23 RCTs with n=6,074. During the intervention: +607 steps/day (95% CI 422–792). After withdrawal: +514 (313–715) pooled across 9 studies. However, only 4 of 18 studies reported benefits after withdrawal by vote count — [BJSM](https://bjsm.bmj.com/content/54/21/1259); [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32672050/). The lead author advises an incentive company (Carrot Insights), a conflict of interest. Luong 2020 found post-intervention effects of SMD 0.10–0.11 with low certainty. Mantzari's review found effects are not sustained long after removal — [IWH summary](https://iwh.on.ca/node/3123).
- Loss-framed incentives (Patel et al., Annals 2016; $1.40 deducted from $42 a month for each missed day) were more effective than gain-framed ones — [Penn Current](https://penncurrent.upenn.edu/2016-03-24/research/prospect-losing-money-pushes-people-achieve-fitness-goals); [CHIBE](https://chibe.upenn.edu/publications/framing-financial-incentives-to-increase-physical-activity-among-overweight-and-obese-adults-a-randomized-controlled-trial/).
- A 2022 smartphone RCT (JMIR) found deposit contracts no more effective than rewards and loss framing less effective than gain framing. **Low uptake** of self-funded deposits was "an important obstacle" to scale — [PubMed 36201384](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36201384/).
- A deposit-contract weight-loss trial (John, Loewenstein, Volpp 2011) found the effect was not significant at follow-up — [CMU PDF](https://www.cmu.edu/dietrich/sds/docs/loewenstein/finIncentExtWeightLoss.pdf).
- **Consumer bet products** (affiliate-blog sources, **[UNVERIFIED]**):
  - DietBet: pooled pot; 4%-in-4-weeks, 10%-in-6-months and maintenance games; fee about 25% on small bets.
  - StepBet: typical $40 for 6 weeks; 15% commission, waived if more than 85% win.
  - HealthyWage: individual weight-loss bets up to $10,000 payouts.
  - Win rates claimed at 62–96% vary by source.
  - Sources: [FinMasters](https://finmasters.com/get-paid-to-lose-weight/); [MyMoneyBlog](https://mymoneyblog.com/dietbet-review.html); [SideHusl](https://sidehusl.com/stepbet/).

**Streaks**
- Silverman & Barasch (J Consumer Research 2023): in experiments with mock exercise apps, streaks increased motivation, but **breaking a streak demotivated** people and could drive them away from the behaviour — [Psychology Today summary](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/blog/ulterior-motives/202306/how-broken-streaks-sap-motivation).
- Duolingo (secondhand, from its 2025 10-K and Q4 letter): about 43M DAU with streaks of 7+ days and about 15M with 365+ days, out of about 52.7M DAU in Q4 2025. Streak-protection tools (freezes) exist. **[UNVERIFIED against filings]** — [viblo.asia](https://viblo.asia/p/duolingo-streak-vi-sao-nguoi-hoc-co-giu-chuoi-ymJXDZ0qJkq); [voxbooster](https://voxbooster.com/vi/blog/duolingo-statistics-2026/).
- A frequently quoted "CHI 2020: 63% higher quit rate after one missed day" claim traces only to blogs. **Do not use** — [ehm-tech blog](https://ehm-tech.pages.dev/habit/blog/habit-streaks-do-they-actually-work).
- A design study of 859 streak designs argues "streaks are about losing" (loss aversion) — [Mobbin](https://mobbin.com/blog/streaks-are-about-losing-a-study-of-859-streak-designs).

**Social comparison backfire**
- In qualitative interviews (arXiv 2025), underperformers felt demotivated when comparison felt unfair or unrepresentative. Some withdrew from the app, hid it, or avoided the leaderboard — [arXiv 2508.12388](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.12388).
- Poncin, Charry & Kullak (2024, two experiments): upward social comparison in online fitness communities produced negative reactions and may reduce app effectiveness, but **stronger ties buffered** the effect (abstract only) — [UCLouvain DIAL](https://dial.uclouvain.be/pr/boreal/en/object/boreal%3A289963).
- Kankanhalli (2015 model): the effect of leaderboard comparison depends on perceived competitive climate and self-efficacy — [HKUST seminar PDF](https://isom.hkust.edu.hk/files/Past_Seminar_List/2015-2016/Atreyi_Kankanhalli_Seminar_29Oct2015.pdf).

### Inferences
Design rules for GRIT:
1. **Default to small groups of 3–8 with fair matchups.** STEP UP used clusters of three, and the Köhler and discrepancy effects reward close-ability partners. Global leaderboards are better replaced by matched-ability brackets, or by "improvement versus your own baseline" scoring so low performers can win.
2. **Add a team-dependency ("weakest-link") mode:** the team goal is met only if every member hits a personal target. This applies the conjunctive Köhler effect. It should come with gentle nudges, not public shaming.
3. **Make streaks forgiving:**
   - freezes and rest days
   - weekly streaks (e.g., "3 workouts per week") rather than daily
   - "streak repair" by doing a make-up session
   - an AI coach message after a break that reframes it ("fresh start")
   This addresses the broken-streak demotivation finding.
4. **Keep money stakes optional, social and short.** Uptake of self-funded deposits is low and the effects fade after the incentive. Short pooled "challenge pots" with friends (DietBet-like), possibly with charity forfeits, fit as an opt-in power feature. The real-money gaming, regulatory and App Store implications are not researched here.
5. **Give the support role (sponsor or buddy) a weekly digest.** STEP UP's support arm was effective during the intervention, and the digest is cheap to build. GRIT's AI coach can write it, e.g., "your buddy missed 2 days — send kudos?"
6. **Plan for decay.** Gamification effects fall roughly two-thirds by about 14 weeks. Rotate seasons and challenges, change teams every 4–6 weeks (as Ladder does with six-week cycles), and rely on identity and community rather than points.

### Gaps
- The search budget ran out before meta-analyses on group-based exercise and social support (e.g., Kassavou 2013 walking groups; Burke 2006 group cohesion) could be retrieved. These are known in the literature but not cited here.
- No primary data on accountability-partner (buddy) apps' retention.
- No research found on BeReal-style simultaneous check-ins for fitness.
- Hydari & Adjerid's Fitbit leaderboard paper (attrition by rank) was seen only as a table of contents.

## 5. Small-group accountability products and hybrid human+AI coaching

### Takeaway
Group and community formats are where the energy is: Strava clubs quadrupled to 1M in 2025, and Ladder's team model raised about $195M. Coach platforms (Trainerize, Everfit, TrueCoach) are cheap B2B tools at $10–$225/mo for coaches. They lack consumer social graphs and only Everfit and Trainerize have basic AI. Future ($199/mo human coaching) abandoned its AI coach in June 2026. The space between pure-AI ($0–$30) and pure-human ($150–$200+) is open for creator- or coach-led groups on top of an AI coach, though demand evidence is thin.

### Cited Findings
- Strava clubs: 1M new clubs in 2025; running clubs ×3.5, hiking clubs ×5.8, club events ×1.5 — [SGB Online](https://sgbonline.com/stravas-year-in-sport-trend-report-highlights-gen-zs-passion-for-run/). Strava removed three club API endpoints on 1 Sep 2026, and public clubs reportedly sit behind login — [Silicon Report](https://www.siliconreport.com/strava-adds-11-99-api-fee-moves-profiles-and-clubs-behind-login-0dedc437f9f054a2).
- Ladder: 17 coach-led teams with six-week programmes at $29.99/mo. Its CEO credits retention to "a supportive community and a path to meaningful progress" (company claim) — [Fortune](https://www.fortune.com/2024/12/10/strength-training-ladder-app-users-growth); [Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250707361917/en).
- Hevy (strength logger):
  - Social features: a following feed plus a Discover feed.
  - Claims 15M+ lifters and 112M+ workouts logged in 2025 (company claim).
  - Instagram and Facebook Stories sharing gave a 42% lift in shares and 25% in virality over 8 weeks (Meta case study, undated).
  - Growth credited to product and low prices.
  - Sources: [Hevy social features](https://www.hevyapp.com/features/social-features/); [Meta case study](https://developers.facebook.com/success-stories/hevy/); [Ottawa Business Journal](https://obj.ca/fitness-app-entrepreneur-pumped-by-hevys-progress-to-2m-in-annual-revenue).
- Coach platforms (2026, from a competitor's comparison, **verify**):
  - TrueCoach: $20/mo for 5 clients, $53 for 20, $107 for 50; no AI builder; strong 1:1 messaging.
  - Everfit: from $19/mo (Pro), about $105/mo (Studio); AI workout builder, food journaling, habits.
  - Trainerize (owned by ABC Fitness): about $10–$20/mo entry, up to $225 for 200 clients; groups, badges, streaks, challenges; branded app is an add-on.
  - None offers a fully white-label app.
  - Source: [FitBudd comparison](https://www.fitbudd.com/insights/everfit-vs-trainerize-vs-truecoach).
- Future: $199/mo for a human coach ($149/mo prepaid annually, $50 first month). It spun out a free AI coaching beta in Feb 2026 and **cancelled it in June 2026** to "double down" on human coaches — [SensAI review, Sept 2026](https://www.sensai.fit/blog/future-app-review-2026); [Athletech News](https://athletechnews.com/age-of-ai-human-personal-trainers-might-become-a-luxury-future-caliber/).
- Hybrid price points: the VASA Fitness gym chain launched an AI plus human hybrid training app at $14.99/mo. Guides estimate $30–$80/mo for hybrid apps versus $480–$1,800/mo for in-person trainers **[low-quality sources]** — [BriefGlance](https://briefglance.com/articles/vasa-fitness-blends-ai-and-human-coaching-in-new-training-app); [FitBudd academy](https://www.fitbudd.com/academy/how-much-personal-trainer-cost-in-gym-vs-online-vs-app); [TrainerFu](https://www.trainerfu.com/blog/ai-personal-trainer-vs-human-trainer-the-honest-2026-comparison/).

### Inferences
- **Opportunity: "GRIT Crews."** Small groups (5–30) run by a coach or creator. The AI coach does the daily personalised work (macro adjustments, plan tweaks, check-ins) and the human leader sets the programme, posts weekly, and runs live or async Q&A. GRIT would take a revenue share. This sits between Ladder (fixed in-house coaches, $30), Trainerize (coach-paid SaaS, no consumer network) and Future ($199 for 1:1).
- For coaches, GRIT can offer a ready social layer, nutrition and food logging, wearable import, and AI that scales a coach to many more clients. Everfit and Trainerize offer only parts of this.
- Future's retreat from AI suggests that for premium buyers the human relationship is what they pay for. GRIT's hybrid should keep the human leader visible and use AI for scale and memory, not as a replacement.
- Hevy's Stories-sharing gains suggest shareable cards (run map plus macros plus streak) are a cheap growth lever.

### Gaps
- There is no hard data on demand for creator-led group coaching in consumer fitness apps: take rates, group sizes, or retention.
- Caliber pricing, Ladder 2026 member count and churn, and Strava club engagement or retention effects were not found.
- No BeReal-style fitness check-in products were identified (search budget exhausted).

## 6. Retention benchmarks for fitness and health apps, and what top retainers do differently

### Takeaway
Health and fitness apps keep roughly 3–8% of installs at day 30. Subscription renewal is among the weakest of any category: RevenueCat 2026 puts the median first annual renewal at about 25% (upper quartile 37%); Adapty puts it last at 30.3%. Peloton, the scaled incumbent, runs about 2% monthly churn but is shrinking. Evidence on what top retainers do comes mostly from company claims (community and teams at Ladder, streaks at Duolingo, social sharing at Hevy) rather than controlled data.

### Cited Findings
- **Day-30 retention for health and fitness:** about 3% (AppsFlyer, 2023) and about 8% from 27% on day 1 (Adjust), via Business of Apps. Other secondary sources cite 4% and 8% — [Business of Apps](https://businessofapps.com/data/health-fitness-app-benchmarks); [Orangesoft, Feb 2026](https://orangesoft.co/blog/strategies-to-increase-fitness-app-engagement-and-retention); [social.plus](https://www.social.plus/blog/to-fitness-app-teams-your-members-need-more-than-workouts-to-stay-motivated). The Business of Apps page could not be fetched to confirm years.
- **RevenueCat State of Subscription Apps 2026** (more than 115k apps), Health & Fitness annual renewal: lower quartile 16%, median 25%, upper quartile 37% (near the bottom of all categories). Monthly retention quartiles were reported as 46/57/68%; another table shows 39/54/64%, with the plan type unclear. **Verify against the report** — [RevenueCat SOSA 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2026/); [RevenueCat renewal rates blog](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/average-subscription-renewal-rates-by-app-category/) (search-summary level; host blocked for fetch).
- **Adapty 2026** (about 16k apps): Health & Fitness is the only category where annual plans dominate revenue (60.6%), and it is last on first-renewal retention at 30.3%. "Annual subscribers almost never return after canceling" (RevenueCat headline coverage) — [Adapty](https://adapty.io/state-of-in-app-subscriptions-2026); [LAVX News](https://news.lavx.hu/article/annual-app-subscribers-almost-never-return-after-canceling-new-revenuecat-report-shows).
- Peloton: Q4 FY26 monthly churn 2.2% (from 1.8%). About half of the involuntary-churn rise came from changed payment-failure email timing, which was then reverted — [The Clip Out](https://theclipout.com/peloton-subscriber-churn-q4-fy26/).
- Zwift's 2024 price rise (+33%) "hasn't helped retention" (commentary) — [the5krunner](https://the5krunner.com/2026/01/23/peak-zwift-2026-indoor-cycling-trends/).
- Duolingo's streak cohort is the clearest at-scale proof of streaks: about 43M of about 52.7M DAU on streaks of 7+ days (secondhand, **[UNVERIFIED]**) — [voxbooster](https://voxbooster.com/vi/blog/duolingo-statistics-2026/).

### Inferences
- With day-30 retention in single digits and annual renewal around 25%, the first 30 days and the first renewal are the battlegrounds. Social graph and group commitments are the most plausible levers, because they add switching costs (friends, crew, history) that pure trackers lack.
- Peloton's payment-email lesson: involuntary churn (failed payments) is material. Dunning and grace periods are low-hanging fruit.
- Because H&F revenue skews annual and annual churners rarely return, GRIT should consider monthly plans and strong win-back offers. Seasonal crew and challenge launches (e.g., spring race season, January) can bring lapsed users back through friends rather than through ads.

### Gaps
- No primary 2025–2026 day-90 benchmark specific to health and fitness was found.
- No controlled data compares retention of social versus non-social fitness apps. The Sensor Tower "State of Mobile Health & Fitness 2025" could not be fetched.
- Strava and Ladder retention figures are not public.
