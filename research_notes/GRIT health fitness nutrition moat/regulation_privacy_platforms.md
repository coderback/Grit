## 1. Medical-device lines: FDA (General Wellness, CDS, enforcement), EU MDR / UK MHRA, EU AI Act

### Takeaway
The FDA's revised General Wellness guidance (6 Jan 2026) widened the US safe harbour: non-invasive estimates of physiologic parameters (including blood pressure) can be "wellness" if the intended use is strictly wellness, values are not presented as clinical, and any "see a professional" nudge names no disease, threshold or abnormality; invasive/minimally-invasive sensing (CGMs) is outside the policy, and diagnosis, treatment, disease and "deficiency"-style claims still make software a device. In the EU/UK, intended purpose decides qualification (MDCG 2019-11 Rev.1, June 2025), and the EU AI Act's high-risk duties attach to AI that is (or is a safety component of) a regulated medical device — now delayed to 2 Aug 2028 — while chatbot transparency (Art. 50) has applied since 2 Aug 2026.

### Cited Findings

**FDA General Wellness (revised Jan 2026)**
- FDA reissued the General Wellness policy as revised final guidance on 6 Jan 2026 with no formal comment period; non-invasive products that estimate or output physiologic parameters, including blood pressure, for wellness uses can qualify as general-wellness products if the intended use is strictly wellness and other conditions are met — [Covington](https://www.cov.com/en/news-and-insights/insights/2026/01/fda-issues-revised-guidance-on-general-wellness-products); [Hardian Health](https://www.hardianhealth.com/insights/fda-wearables-guidelines-update-2026); [Loeb QuickTakes](https://quicktakes.loeb.com/post/102m1yw/fda-updates-its-general-wellness-device-frameworkwhat-companies-need-to-know)
- Commentators stress it is "not a green light for blood pressure claims"; features that mimic clinical values still need validation — [fdaatty.com](https://fdaatty.com/fda-general-wellness-guidance-2026-what-wearable-and-digital-health-companies-can-and-cant-do-now/); [Hardian Health](https://www.hardianhealth.com/insights/fda-wearables-guidelines-update-2026)
- Invasive products (those piercing/penetrating skin or mucous membranes) are excluded; a glucose product using "minimally invasive microneedle technology" is not low-risk even with purely wellness claims — [Troutman Pepper Locke](https://www.troutman.com/insights/fdas-2026-guidance-on-general-wellness-devices-policy-for-low-risk-devices/); [King & Spalding](https://www.kslaw.com/insights/articles/fda-updates-general-wellness-and-clinical-decision-support-guidance-documents)
- A wearable may notify users that evaluation by a health care professional "may be helpful" only if the notification does not name a specific disease, characterize output as abnormal or diagnostic, include clinical thresholds or treatment recommendations, or provide ongoing disease-management alerts — [Covington](https://www.cov.com/en/news-and-insights/insights/2026/01/fda-issues-revised-guidance-on-general-wellness-products); [Greenberg Traurig](https://www.gtlaw.com/en/insights/2026/1/fda-updates-two-digital-health-final-guidance-documents)
- Firms read the guidance's CGM example as suggesting that AI using data *from* a (separately cleared) CGM to provide nutritional guidance could be wellness, even though the CGM sensor itself is not — [Emergo by UL](https://www.emergobyul.com/news/fda-updates-broaden-wellness-and-clinical-decision-support-software-devices-under-enforcement); [King & Spalding](https://www.kslaw.com/insights/articles/fda-updates-general-wellness-and-clinical-decision-support-guidance-documents)
- FDA has not clarified what "range" it considers appropriate for wellness outputs — [Greenberg Traurig](https://www.gtlaw.com/en/insights/2026/1/fda-updates-two-digital-health-final-guidance-documents)
- A separate draft guidance (23 Jan 2026) covers cuffless blood-pressure devices outside the wellness policy and the clinical evidence expected — [Hardian Health](https://www.hardianhealth.com/insights/fda-wearables-guidelines-update-2026)

**Enforcement example: WHOOP Blood Pressure Insights**
- FDA warning letter (dated 14/15 July 2025) said WHOOP's "Blood Pressure Insights" was an uncleared device and that BP estimation "is not a low-risk function" so could not be general wellness even if intended for general health — [MobiHealthNews](https://www.mobihealthnews.com/news/whoop-receives-warning-letter-fda-blood-pressure-insights); [Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/health-law-and-business/whoop-gets-fda-warning-on-blood-pressure-feature-marketing)
- In late June 2026 FDA sent a close-out letter saying it no longer intends to enforce, citing WHOOP's changes to the product/labeling (e.g., adjusting dial boundaries so the feature does not appear to classify BP clinically) and the updated wellness guidance; this is non-enforcement, not a finding that the feature is a non-device — [MedTech Dive](https://medtechdive.com/news/fda-drops-whoop-warning-letter-over-blood-pressure-feature/823652); [MassDevice](https://www.massdevice.com/fda-closes-warning-letter-whoop-blood-pressure/); [Fierce Biotech](https://www.fiercebiotech.com/medtech/fda-drops-blood-pressure-device-enforcement-against-whoop)

**Examples of products that needed clearance**
- Apple Watch Hypertension Notifications: FDA 510(k) K250507 cleared 11 Sept 2025; algorithm reviews 30 days of optical-sensor data and alerts on consistent signs of hypertension; trained on >100,000 participants and validated in a >2,000-person study — [Innolitics 510k DB](https://510k.innolitics.com/search/K250507); [9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2025/09/11/apple-watch-hypertension-alerts-feature-receives-fda-clearance/); [HLTH](https://hlth.com/insights/news/apple-receives-fda-nod-for-hypertension-notification-feature-2025-09-16)
- Signos: FDA cleared an OTC glucose-monitoring system for weight management (Aug 2025), pairing Dexcom Stelo with an AI platform — first OTC system cleared for weight management — [Fierce Biotech](https://www.fiercebiotech.com/medtech/fda-clears-signos-over-counter-cgm-powered-weight-loss-app); [Drugs.com](https://www.drugs.com/news/fda-clears-otc-glucose-monitoring-weight-management-126380.html)
- Natural Cycles: De Novo granted Aug 2018 as a Class II device (first contraception app); 2019 final order set special controls (software V&V, hazard analysis, clinical performance, human factors, labeling) — [BioSpace](https://www.biospace.com/fda-releases-final-classification-for-natural-cycles-the-first-and-only-birth-control-app-in-the-u-s); [RAPS](https://www.raps.org/resource/cdrh-classifies-software-app-for-contraception-int.html)

**FDA Clinical Decision Support (revised Jan 2026)**
- Revised CDS guidance (reported 6 Jan 2026) lets clinician-facing software give a single recommendation where it is the only clinically appropriate option (2022 version effectively required a list); FDA declined to define "clinically appropriate" — [Covington](https://www.cov.com/news-and-insights/insights/2026/01/5-key-takeaways-from-fdas-revised-clinical-decision-support-cds-software-guidance); [JD Supra](https://www.jdsupra.com/legalnews/ringing-in-the-new-year-with-digital-5307049/)
- The CDS guidance stays clinician-focused; FDA announced no new policy for consumer-facing tools such as patient decision support, health chatbots or symptom checkers — [Nixon Law Group](https://www.nixonlawgroup.com/resources/fda-relaxes-clinical-decision-support-and-general-wellness-guidance-what-it-means-for-generative-ai-and-consumer-wearables); [Covington](https://www.cov.com/news-and-insights/insights/2026/01/5-key-takeaways-from-fdas-revised-clinical-decision-support-cds-software-guidance)
- Date conflict: most sources say 6 Jan 2026; one says 29 Jan; another says formally published 11 Mar 2026 — [search summary of medaidirectory / evidencemd](https://medaidirectory.com/blog/fda-clinical-decision-support-guidance-2026)

**EU MDR / UK MHRA**
- MDCG 2019-11 Rev.1 (software qualification/classification under MDR/IVDR) published 17 June 2025, the day after MDCG 2025-4 (online platforms for medical device software) — [European Commission](https://health.ec.europa.eu/latest-updates/update-mdcg-2019-11-rev1-qualification-and-classification-software-regulation-eu-2017745-and-2025-06-17_en); [Emergo by UL](https://www.emergobyul.com/news/european-revision-primary-software-guidance-mdcg-2019-11-revision-1-small-changes-meaningful)
- Qualification depends on the manufacturer's intended purpose, not how the software works — [OpenRegulatory](https://openregulatory.com/mdcg/mdcg-2019-11)
- Sources disagree on how much Rev.1 changed Rule 11 (one says no change to qualification logic; others describe added examples incl. illness-prevention software) — [Emergo](https://www.emergobyul.com/news/european-revision-primary-software-guidance-mdcg-2019-11-revision-1-small-changes-meaningful); [NSF](https://www.nsf.org/au/en/life-science-news/mdcg-2019-11-rev-1-important-updates-for-medical-device-software-mdsw-manufacturers)

- Great Britain: software/AI meeting the device definition is governed by the UK Medical Devices Regulations 2002 (SI 2002/618), not EU MDR; new post-market surveillance requirements came into force June 2025; MHRA said it would publish a dedicated AI-as-a-medical-device framework in 2026 (publication not confirmed) — [Taylor Wessing](https://www.taylorwessing.com/de/synapse/2025/ai-in-life-sciences/uk-approach-to-regulating-ai-in-life-sciences); [RegDesk](https://www.regdesk.co/blog/mhra-guidance-on-software-and-ai-as-a-medical-device/)
- MHRA AI Airlock sandbox Phase 2 concluded May 2026 — [Fieldfisher](https://www.fieldfisher.com/en/insights/mhra-ai-airlock-from-pilot-sandbox-to-scaling-regulatory-pathway-for-ai-medical-devices); [GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/government/collections/ai-airlock-the-regulatory-sandbox-for-aiamd)

**EU AI Act (incl. Digital Omnibus on AI, 2026)**
- Digital Omnibus on AI adopted as Regulation (EU) 2026/1744; Council final approval 29 June 2026; published in OJ 24 July 2026, in force 27 July 2026 — [AIActo](https://www.aiacto.eu/en/blog/digital-omnibus-ai-act-report-2027); [CSA](https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/research/csa-research-note-eu-ai-act-omnibus-vii-deadline-delay-20260/)
- Annex III (stand-alone) high-risk obligations now apply from 2 Dec 2027 (was 2 Aug 2026); Annex I product-embedded AI, including medical devices, from 2 Aug 2028 (was 2 Aug 2027); no medical-device carve-out — [Legalithm tracker](https://www.legalithm.com/en/blog/ai-act-medical-devices-deadlines-omnibus-tracker); [Freshfields](https://www.freshfields.com/en/our-thinking/blogs/technology-quotient/eu-ai-act-unpacked-34-the-final-digital-omnibus-on-ai-key-amendments-to-the-a-102nber); [Specculo](https://specculo.com/eu-ai-act-omnibus-hrai-delay-medical-devices/)

### Inferences
- **US do/don't wording line (post-Jan-2026):**
  - OK (wellness): "track your sleep/HRV/recovery," "support a healthy weight," "build habits," "readiness score," "your resting HR is higher than your 30-day baseline," "consider talking to a professional if you're concerned."
  - Device territory: "diagnose," "detect [disease]," "screen for," "treat," "manage your diabetes/hypertension/PCOS," "iron/B12/vitamin D deficiency," "pre-diabetic," "abnormal," "hypertensive range," "your BP indicates stage 1 hypertension," "adjust your GLP-1 dose," "safe to take with," "fertile window for contraception." Anything that names a disease or uses clinical cut-offs (e.g., ≥130/80 mmHg, HbA1c ≥5.7%) is the bright line the 2026 guidance draws.
- **HRV/recovery:** low risk if framed as fitness readiness/stress/recovery; avoid AFib, arrhythmia, illness-detection or "you may be getting sick" claims (Apple's AFib and hypertension features required clearance).
- **Blood pressure:** GRIT only *displaying/trending* user-entered or cuff-measured BP read from HealthKit/Health Connect is low-risk; *interpreting* it against clinical categories or alerting is where WHOOP got a warning letter. The WHOOP close-out shows FDA accepts BP outputs that avoid clinical categorisation, but this is enforcement discretion, not a safe harbour.
- **CGM data:** reading glucose from a cleared CGM (Stelo/Lingo via HealthKit) and giving *nutrition* feedback ("this meal produced a larger rise than your usual breakfast") appears to fit the 2026 CGM example; claims about pre-diabetes, insulin resistance, diabetes management, or "weight-loss system" claims tied to CGM put GRIT where Signos needed 510(k).
- **Menstrual cycle tracking:** period logging and cycle-phase-aware training are wellness; fertility-window predictions for contraception (Natural Cycles) or conception claims, or detecting PCOS/endometriosis/perimenopause, are device claims.
- **Micronutrient insights:** "you logged less iron than the reference intake this week" (dietary intake vs. RDI) is a nutrition/wellness statement; "you are iron deficient/anaemic" or interpreting lab values against reference ranges is a disease/diagnostic claim. Lab-result import + AI interpretation is the highest-risk planned feature (closest to CDS/diagnostic, and patient-facing CDS gets no new FDA relief).
- **GLP-1 logging:** logging doses, side effects, protein/hydration reminders is wellness/journaling; dose recommendations, titration advice or "pairs with your semaglutide" sales copy would implicate device and drug-promotion rules (see FDA GLP-1 telehealth crackdown in section 4).
- **AI coach:** an LLM that answers free-form health questions can drift into diagnosis/treatment outputs regardless of marketing; guardrails (system prompt + output classifier) that refuse diagnosis/dosing and redirect are what keep "intended use" credible.
- **EU:** for a consumer wellness app not qualified as a medical device, the AI Act's high-risk regime largely does not bite; Article 50 (disclose AI interaction) does apply now to GRIT's coach chat in the EU. If GRIT ever ships a qualified device feature with AI, Annex I high-risk duties apply from 2 Aug 2028 on top of MDR (likely Class IIa+ under Rule 11, needing a notified body).

## 2. Health-data privacy: HIPAA, FTC (HBNR + Section 5), US states, GDPR/UK GDPR, Nigeria, reproductive data

### Takeaway
HIPAA almost certainly does not apply to GRIT (a direct-to-consumer app that is not a covered entity or business associate), but that leaves GRIT under the FTC (Section 5 + Health Breach Notification Rule, where "breach" includes unauthorized sharing, e.g., to ad pixels/SDKs), state consumer-health-data laws (Washington MHMDA with a private right of action, Nevada, Connecticut), CIPA-style wiretap class actions (Flo/Meta verdict), and GDPR/UK GDPR Article 9 (health data = special category, in practice explicit consent). Nigeria's NDPA 2023 + GAID (effective 19 Sept 2025) adds DPIA, registration and 72-hour breach duties.

### Cited Findings

**FTC Health Breach Notification Rule and enforcement**
- 2024 HBNR amendments took effect 29 July 2024; they make clear health apps are covered and that a "breach" includes an unauthorized disclosure the company itself makes (e.g., sharing health data with ad platforms), not only cyberattacks; notice due without unreasonable delay and within 60 calendar days — [National Law Review](https://natlawreview.com/article/ftc-reverses-course-health-app-privacy); [HIPAA Journal](https://www.hipaajournal.com/ftc-rescinds-policy-statement-health-app-data-breaches/)
- 9 Sept 2026: FTC rescinded its 2021 policy statement that had extended HBNR to health apps, saying it was superseded by the 2024 rule update; commentators disagree on the effect — some say unauthorized sharing alone no longer triggers HBNR, others say the 2024 rule text still covers most non-HIPAA health apps; FTC Section 5 authority is unaffected — [AHA News](https://www.aha.org/news/headline/2026-09-10-ftc-rescinds-policy-statement-extending-breach-notification-rule-apps-devices-collect-health); [Epstein Becker Green](https://www.ebglaw.com/health-law-advisor/ftc-reverses-course-on-health-app-privacy); [Hunton](https://www.hunton.com/privacy-and-cybersecurity-law-blog/ftc-rescinds-2021-policy-statement-on-health-app-data-breaches); [The Useful Daily](https://www.theusefuldaily.com/article/ftc-health-app-breach-rule-still-applies-2026-09-18)
- GoodRx (Feb 2023): first HBNR action; $1.5M civil penalty; permanent ban on sharing health info with third parties for advertising; affirmative express consent required for other sharing; dark-pattern consent banned — [FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2023/02/ftc-enforcement-action-bar-goodrx-sharing-consumers-sensitive-health-info-advertising)
- Premom (May 2023): FTC's second HBNR action; barred from sharing health data for advertising; consent required for other sharing — [FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2023/05/ovulation-tracking-app-premom-will-be-barred-sharing-health-data-advertising-under-proposed-ftc)
- BetterHelp (final July 2023): $7.8M for refunds; ban on sharing personal info for re-targeting; affirmative express consent; privacy program; deletion directives to third parties; retention schedule; brought under Section 5, not HBNR — [FTC final order](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2023/07/ftc-gives-final-approval-order-banning-betterhelp-sharing-sensitive-health-data-advertising); [Wilson concurrence](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/commissioner_wilson_concur_betterhelp_3.2.23.pdf)

### Inferences
- **HIPAA:** GRIT is not a covered entity/business associate unless it contracts with providers/health plans/employers' plans to deliver services on their behalf (e.g., a clinic-prescribed GLP-1 program or a payer partnership) — at that point a BAA and HIPAA programme are needed. Lab-result import directly from the user does not create HIPAA status.
- **Ad tech is the #1 enforcement vector.** Every major case (GoodRx, BetterHelp, Premom, Flo, Amazon SDK) is about health signals flowing to ad/analytics SDKs. Do: no Meta/TikTok/Google ads SDKs or pixels in authenticated health screens; server-side analytics with event names stripped of health content ("meal_logged" not "logged_ozempic_dose"); no custom audiences built from health users. Don't: let a marketing SDK see app-open events from a period/GLP-1/lab screen.
- **Under WA MHMDA, nearly everything GRIT collects is "consumer health data"** (meal photos, weight, workouts, cycle, GLP-1, inferred data), so: separate consent to collect and to share, a standalone Consumer Health Data Privacy Policy, no geofencing near health facilities, and treat any sale as needing signed authorization (see Gaps — unverified specifics). Private right of action makes this the most litigable US regime.
- **Reproductive and GLP-1 data:** with the HIPAA repro rule vacated, consumer apps rely on state law; design cycle tracking for minimal server retention (on-device or E2E-encrypted option, short retention, no location linkage, no social feed sharing by default) to reduce subpoena and breach exposure.
- **GDPR/UK GDPR:** practically all GRIT data is Art. 9 health data → explicit consent (granular toggles per category: health-platform sync, meal photo AI analysis, coach memory, cycle tracking, model training). DPIA required. An EU representative (Art. 27) and UK representative if no establishment. International transfers to US vendors under DPF/SCCs.
- **AI coach memory notes** ("knee injury," "on GLP-1," "history of binge eating") are inferred/derived health data under GDPR and MHMDA — must be viewable, editable and deletable by the user, and included in DSAR exports.
- **Training ML models on user data:** EU — legitimate interest is not available for Art. 9 data; use opt-in explicit consent or truly anonymised data; US — FTC has ordered algorithm deletion in past cases (background knowledge, see Gaps), so treat training as a separate disclosed purpose with opt-in for health data; California sensitive-PI risk assessment required.
- **Nigeria:** register with NDPC if GRIT crosses the major-importance thresholds, conduct and DPO-vet a DPIA (health + profiling), localise-or-justify cross-border transfers, 72-hour breach notice.

## 3. Platform rules: Apple App Store / HealthKit and Google Play / Health Connect

### Takeaway
Apple prohibits using HealthKit data for advertising/marketing/data mining, storing personal health information in iCloud, and (since Nov 2025, guideline 5.1.2(i)) sharing personal data with third-party AI without clear disclosure and explicit permission — directly relevant to GRIT sending meals/chats to Azure OpenAI. Google Play requires an accurate Health apps declaration, a privacy policy identical to the one shown in Health Connect, and hardware disclosure for device-dependent features; medical-claim apps face greater scrutiny on both stores.

### Cited Findings

## 4. AI-specific rules: liability, consumer protection, vendor data handling, training, EU AI Act transparency

### Takeaway
There is no federal US "AI health advice" statute, but FTC Section 5 (deception/unfairness), state laws banning AI "therapy" or AI implying licensure (Illinois, Nevada, Utah, California AB 489), the EU AI Act Article 50 disclosure duty (live since 2 Aug 2026) and Apple 5.1.2(i) together require: disclose it is AI, never present it as a clinician, avoid mental-health-therapy positioning, get explicit consent before sending personal data to the model vendor, and keep claims about AI accuracy substantiated. Azure OpenAI ("Foundry Models sold by Azure") does not give prompts to OpenAI or use them for training, but default abuse monitoring may retain flagged samples; Global deployments may process data in any region.

### Cited Findings
- Microsoft: prompts and completions for Models sold by Azure are not available to other customers or to OpenAI/other model providers and are not used to train/improve foundation models without permission; models are stateless — [Microsoft Learn: Data, privacy and security for Foundry Models sold by Azure](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/foundry/responsible-ai/openai/data-privacy)
- Abuse monitoring: when indicators are detected, samples of prompts/completions may be selected for (mostly automated, sometimes human) review; customers can apply for Modified Abuse Monitoring to avoid storage/human review; approval is discretionary (BAA does not guarantee it) — [Microsoft Learn: abuse monitoring](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/foundry/openai/concepts/abuse-monitoring); [Microsoft Q&A](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-in/answers/questions/6053949/azure-openai-data-zone-standard-us-under-a-hipaa-b); [Microsoft Q&A](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-ca/answers/questions/5930329/azure-openai-gating-team-for-hipaa-modified-abuse)
- Historical default abuse-monitoring retention was up to 30 days; one Q&A notes May 2026 docs no longer list a period — [Microsoft Q&A](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-in/answers/questions/6053949/azure-openai-data-zone-standard-us-under-a-hipaa-b)
- Stateful features (Responses API stored history, Assistants threads, stored completions) retain data by design — [Microsoft Learn data privacy](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/foundry/responsible-ai/openai/data-privacy)
- Global deployments may process prompts in any Foundry region; US Data Zone keeps abuse-monitoring data at rest in the US — [Microsoft Q&A](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/answers/questions/5966351/azure-ai-foundry-modified-abuse-monitoring-eu-data); [Microsoft Q&A](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-in/answers/questions/6053949/azure-openai-data-zone-standard-us-under-a-hipaa-b)

- **Azure configuration checklist:** use a Data Zone (EU Data Zone for EU/UK users if feasible; US Data Zone otherwise) rather than Global deployment; avoid stateful APIs (Assistants/stored completions) or set retention controls; apply for Modified Abuse Monitoring (realistic only with a managed Azure relationship); sign Microsoft DPA (GDPR processor terms) — a BAA is only needed if GRIT becomes a HIPAA business associate. Terms of service should include disclaimers and limitation of liability (enforceability varies; lawyer needed).

## 5. Eating-disorder safety, minors, weight-loss content, age assurance

### Takeaway
The UK Online Safety Act (children's codes in force 25 July 2025) treats eating-disorder content as "primary priority content" that services with user-to-user features (GRIT's social feed) must prevent children of any age from encountering, typically via highly effective age assurance where such content is not banned outright. In the US, Texas's App Store Accountability Act is enforceable after the Supreme Court declined to block it (July 2026), adding age-signal and parental-consent duties for developers. GRIT's safest posture is 18+ (or 16+/18+ by region) with calorie floors and ED-content moderation.

### Cited Findings
- Ofcom Protection of Children Codes: compliance required from 25 July 2025; eating-disorder content is "primary priority content" alongside pornography, suicide and self-harm; services must prevent children of any age encountering it and, where not banned for everyone, use highly effective age assurance (OSA s.12) — [Pinsent Masons Out-Law](https://www.pinsentmasons.com/en-gb/out-law/news/uk-childrens-safety-codes-published); [DRCF quick guide](https://www-bau.drcf.org.uk/online-safety/illegal-and-harmful-content/quick-guide-to-childrens-safety-codes); [Ofcom enforcement bulletin](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/online-safety/protecting-children/enforcement-bulletin-enforcement-programme-to-protect-children-from-harmful-content-through-the-use-of-age-assurance)
- Ofcom opened an age-assurance enforcement programme on 24 July 2025 targeting services hosting PPC, explicitly mentioning small risky services hosting eating-disorder content — [Ofcom enforcement bulletin](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/online-safety/protecting-children/enforcement-bulletin-enforcement-programme-to-protect-children-from-harmful-content-through-the-use-of-age-assurance)
- Texas SB 2420 (App Store Accountability Act): preliminarily enjoined 23 Dec 2025; Fifth Circuit stayed injunction 28 May 2026; Supreme Court denied emergency applications 6 July 2026 so the law is enforceable while the merits appeal continues; developers face age-verification signal, parental-consent (downloads/in-app purchases) and rating/disclosure duties; app stores must age-verify existing accounts by 1 Oct 2027 — [MoFo](https://www.mofo.com/resources/insights/251111-texas-targets-app-stores-with-new-accountability-law); [Lexology](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=60cfedda-77a9-4539-b439-a68c4f42b52a); [Sourcepoint](https://sourcepoint.com/blog/texas-app-store-accountability-act-injunction-lifted-and-calprivacy-issues-consumer-guidance/)
- FTC 6(b) inquiry into AI chatbots focuses on effects on children/teens and age-based access limits — [DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiper.com/en/insights/publications/2025/09/ftc-ai-chatbots)

### Inferences
- **Do:** age gate at signup (18+ recommended, given weight-loss/calorie features and social feed); consume Apple Declared Age Range / Google Play Age Signals where available (needed for Texas); hard floors on AI-suggested calorie targets (commonly cited clinical heuristics like ~1,200 kcal women / ~1,500 kcal men — clinical, not a legal standard; have a dietitian set them) and cap weekly loss rate (~1% bodyweight/week); refuse targets for BMI < 18.5 or declared ED history; screen onboarding for ED risk and route to resources (e.g., NEDA/Beat); moderate the social feed for pro-ED content ("thinspo," extreme fasting, purging) with keyword + image classifiers and reporting.
- **Don't:** allow under-18s to set weight-loss goals or join weight-loss stakes challenges; show before/after body photos or leaderboards ranked by weight lost to minors; let the AI coach praise extreme deficits or fasting streaks.
- **UK:** because GRIT has user-to-user features, it is in OSA scope; if minors can access, a children's risk assessment and age assurance are required. An 18+ policy with highly effective age assurance, or banning ED content for all users and enforcing it, are the two compliant postures. UK OSA scoping needs a lawyer.

## 6. Financial-incentive features (stakes/betting on goals): gambling law

### Takeaway
Paid "stake on your goal" features sit in a grey zone: US operators (HealthyWage, DietBet) frame them as contests of skill and issue 1099s, but this search found no regulator rulings; the legal analysis is state-by-state (prize + consideration + chance), and the UK Gambling Act 2005 and Nigerian lottery/gaming law are separate regimes. This is a "needs a gaming lawyer before launch" feature; non-cash or charity-donation stakes are the low-risk alternative.

### Cited Findings
- HealthyWage describes winnings as contest winnings taxed as 1099 income; participants generally split the pot (minus fees) with other winners; some offer no-lose guarantees — [MoneyCrashers HealthyWage review](https://www.moneycrashers.com/healthywage-review/); [FinMasters](https://finmasters.com/get-paid-to-lose-weight/)
- Critics characterise weight-wagering as close to gambling, and a 2013 Inquirer column questioned whether these are "online gambling operations in disguise" — [The Outline](https://theoutline.com/post/8392/weight-loss-wagering-healthywage-dietbet); [Philadelphia Inquirer](https://www.inquirer.com/philly/news/columnists/20130414_Dieting_for_Dollars.html)

### Inferences
- Skill-vs-chance argument is strongest when outcomes depend on the user's own effort (logging workouts) and weakest when pools pay out based on others' failure rates or unverifiable data. Pooled "losers pay winners" designs look more like wagering than flat-fee refund commitments.
- Low-risk alternatives: (a) commitment devices where forfeited money goes to charity (not to GRIT or other users); (b) GRIT-funded prizes with no purchase necessary (sweepstakes rules); (c) badges/streak insurance with in-app currency that has no cash-out (but check Apple/Google rules on loot-box-like mechanics).
- Apple/Google both restrict real-money gaming and contests (Apple requires sponsor be the developer, official rules, and no IAP for entry — background knowledge, unverified here); app-store review is a gating risk in addition to state law.
- Weight-loss stakes also interact with ED risk (section 5) — exclude minors and low-BMI users.

### Gaps
- No primary-source legal analysis found: state skill-game tests (e.g., dominant-factor vs. material-element), states that restrict paid-entry skill contests (commonly cited: AZ, AR, CT, DE, LA, MT, SC, SD, TN — unverified), UK Gambling Act 2005 s.6/s.14 betting/prize-competition definitions, Nigeria National Lottery Act / state gaming laws.
- Apple guideline 5.3 (gaming, gambling and lotteries) current text not verified.
- No enforcement action against HealthyWage/DietBet-type apps found.

## 7. Synthesis: what GRIT can build safely vs. what crosses into regulated territory
