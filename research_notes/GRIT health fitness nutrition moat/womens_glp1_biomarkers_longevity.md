# Women's health, GLP-1 support, biomarkers, CGM, blood pressure and longevity: competitive landscape for GRIT (as of Nov 2026)

Scope note: researched 5 Nov 2026 with web search. Many pricing and feature figures come from comparison or affiliate blogs and conflict with each other; these are flagged. "REG FLAG" marks a feature that could make GRIT a regulated medical device or a health-records handler (for the regulation researcher). Items not confirmed by a source are in Gaps.

## 1. Cycle and women's health (Flo, Clue, Stardust, Natural Cycles, wearables, Wild.AI/FitrWoman, menopause apps)

### Takeaway
Strong evidence says cycle phase has at most a trivial effect on strength, hypertrophy and performance, so "cycle syncing" training plans are mostly marketing. What is better supported is small, predictable luteal-phase changes in appetite, energy expenditure and water weight, and that is exactly what an adaptive calorie algorithm and a coach can use. Privacy is the biggest risk in the category: Flo has paid settlements and Meta lost a jury verdict over Flo data. Menopause is the fast-growing, well-funded part of women's health (Midi reached unicorn status; WeightWatchers launched a menopause programme), and wearables (Oura, WHOOP, Apple) are moving into it quickly.

### Cited Findings
**Evidence on cycle-phase training**
- McNulty et al. 2020 (Sports Medicine) meta-analysis: exercise performance "might be trivially reduced during the early follicular phase" compared with other phases. — [PMC7497427](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7497427/)
- Colenso-Semple et al. 2023 umbrella review (Frontiers in Sports and Active Living) found "scant low-quality and largely inconsistent evidence" of differences between phases in strength, performance or hypertrophy. Its title: "Current evidence shows no influence of women's menstrual cycle phase on acute strength performance or adaptations to resistance exercise training." — [Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/sports-and-active-living/articles/10.3389/fspor.2023.1054542/full); [PMC10076834](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10076834/)
- I did not find a 2025–2026 umbrella review that changes this conclusion.

**Cycle effects on appetite, energy and weight (relevant to adaptive calorie targets)**
- A review in the International Journal of Obesity reports that in the luteal phase energy intake and energy expenditure both rise, with more cravings for high-carbohydrate and high-fat foods. — [Nature/IJO](https://www.nature.com/articles/0803699)
- A sleep-metabolism study of 9 women found core temperature +0.27°C and sleeping energy expenditure +6.9% in the luteal phase. — [PMC6981303](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6981303/)
- Secondary sources put the luteal resting energy expenditure increase at roughly 50–100 kcal/day, though not every study finds it. — [ResearchGate summary of IJO review](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/6154046_Impact_of_the_menstrual_cycle_on_determinants_of_energy_balance_A_putative_role_in_weight_loss_attempts)
- Counter-evidence: a 2025 medRxiv preprint reports that appetite and energy intake "remain consistent across menstrual cycle phases when using precise measurement methods" (not peer-reviewed). — [medRxiv 2025](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.02.28.25323098.full.pdf)
- A pilot proteomics study in active women found no phase differences in appetite hormones, subjective appetite or energy intake. — [PMC10879539](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10879539/)
- Popular coverage cites about 0.5 kg of weight gain around menstruation from water retention (2023 study of 42 people; original not verified). — [Medical News Today](https://www.medicalnewstoday.com/articles/327326)

### Inferences
- GRIT's adaptive calorie algorithm (weight-trend based) will misread pre-menstrual water weight (about 0.5–2 kg) as fat gain or a stall, and may cut calories at the wrong time. A cycle-aware trend filter (detect or let users log period start, discount the luteal/menstrual window, explain "this is water") is a concrete, evidence-based improvement. No major calorie tracker found in this research does this explicitly (not confirmed; needs a product check of MacroFactor, MFP and Lose It).
- The coach can honestly say "slightly more hunger and about 50–100 kcal higher energy needs in the luteal phase is common; flex your target a little" rather than promising phase-specific workout plans. Not over-claiming cycle syncing is a credibility advantage over Wild.AI-style products.
- Privacy as a feature: no third-party ad or analytics SDKs on cycle screens, on-device or end-to-end encrypted cycle data, an option to keep cycle data out of the social graph and the AI coach's shared memory, and clear export and delete. After Flo/Meta and with Washington's My Health My Data Act-type laws, this is a requirement, not a differentiator only. REG FLAG: state consumer health data laws (WA MHMDA, Nevada) and post-Dobbs subpoena risk.
- Perimenopause is under-served by fitness apps: symptoms (sleep, hot flushes, joint pain), body composition change, and strength training for bone and muscle fit GRIT's existing habits, workouts and coach. A "40+ women" track (protein, lifting, sleep, symptom log feeding the coach) is a gap between clinical players (Midi, WW) and symptom trackers (Balance).
- Contraception or fertility predictions would make GRIT a medical device (Natural Cycles is regulated). REG FLAG. GRIT should not predict fertile windows.

- Apple Watch cycle features (retrospective ovulation estimates via wrist temperature) not confirmed from Apple sources in this research.

## 2. GLP-1 users: numbers, needs and competitor apps

### Takeaway
About 12% of US adults currently take a GLP-1 (KFF, Nov 2025), up from 6% in May 2024, and roughly 1.5–2M+ UK adults use them privately, mostly tirzepatide. Pills (Wegovy pill from Jan 2026, Lilly's Foundayo/orforglipron from April 2026) and cash prices from $149/month are widening access. Users need protein, strength training to protect lean mass, side-effect and dose logging, and above all a plan for maintenance after stopping, since about 60% of lost weight returns within a year. Dedicated trackers (Shotsy) do dose logging well but are thin on nutrition and training; nutrition apps (MFP, Noom) have bolted on GLP-1 modes; telehealth (Hims, Ro, WW, Noom Med) owns the prescription. Nobody owns the full "on-drug + strength + social + off-ramp" journey.

### Cited Findings
**User numbers**
- KFF Health Tracking Poll (fielded 27 Oct–2 Nov 2025, n=1,350): 12% of US adults currently take a GLP-1 and 18% have ever taken one. In May 2024 current use was 6%. Use peaks at ages 50–64 (22%); women 15% vs men 9%; 45% of adults with diabetes. — [KFF](https://www.kff.org/public-opinion/kff-health-tracking-poll-prescription-drug-costs-views-on-trump-administration-actions-and-glp-1-use/); [KFF May 2024](https://www.kff.org/health-costs/kff-health-tracking-poll-may-2024-the-publics-use-and-views-of-glp-1-drugs/)
- A 2026 Gallup survey reportedly found 11% using them for weight loss specifically (cited secondhand, not verified against Gallup). — [glplens](https://glplens.com/learn/how-many-americans-take-glp-1s/)
- UK: UCL study (BMC Medicine, 2026) estimates 1.6M adults in England, Scotland and Wales used GLP-1s for weight loss between early 2024 and early 2025; 4 in 5 were on tirzepatide. Nesta estimates 1.5M private users; the Food Foundation (Apr 2026) gives 1.4–1.6M. IQVIA data reportedly show over 2M paying privately by July 2025, about 7 times NHS numbers (secondary). — [Nesta](https://www.nesta.org.uk/data-visualisation-and-interactive/silver-bullet-or-sticking-plaster-weight-loss-drugs-and-the-uks-obesity-crisis/); [Food Foundation briefing Apr 2026](https://foodfoundation.org.uk/sites/default/files/2026-04/The%20GLP-1%20Revolution_briefing_v4_0.pdf); [heyslim (secondary)](https://www.heyslim.co.uk/blog/uk-weight-loss-medication-statistics-2026)
- Bain projects UK consumer GLP-1 use rising from 3% in 2026 to 5% in 2030. — [The Grocer](https://www.thegrocer.co.uk/news/glp-1-weight-loss-drug-use-set-to-double-by-2030/725511.article)

**Oral and compounded versions**
- FDA approved the Wegovy pill (oral semaglutide) on 22 Dec 2025; US availability from 5 Jan 2026. Cash price at launch: $149/month for 1.5 mg and 4 mg, $299 for 9 mg and 25 mg. The 4 mg $149 price ran until 15 Apr 2026, then $199. — [Novo Nordisk](https://www.novonordisk-us.com/media/press-releases/wegovy-pill-fda-approval-obesity.html); [Fierce Pharma](https://www.fiercepharma.com/pharma/novos-wegovy-pill-makes-us-debut-starter-dose-launching-149-month-cash-paying-patients); [Ro](https://ro.co/weight-loss/oral-wegovy/)
- FDA approved Lilly's orforglipron (brand Foundayo) on 1 Apr 2026. As a small molecule it has no food or water timing restrictions. Pre-approval reports said $149 starting dose and up to $399 cash for higher doses (final prices not confirmed). — [Medscape](https://www.medscape.com/viewarticle/fda-okays-first-orforglipron-weight-management-2026a10009z5); [Drugs.com](https://www.drugs.com/history/foundayo.html); [CNN](https://edition.cnn.com/2025/12/22/health/wegovy-pill-glp1-weight-loss-drugs)
- Compounded GLP-1s: shortages ended (tirzepatide Dec 2024, semaglutide Feb 2025). On 30 Apr 2026 FDA proposed permanently excluding semaglutide, tirzepatide and liraglutide from the 503B bulks list (comment period to 29 Jun 2026; final outcome not found). 503A compounding remains a route. FDA sent about 30 warning letters to telehealth firms in March 2026 and 25 more on 16 Jun 2026 (dates vary by source). FDA had 990 adverse event reports for compounded semaglutide and 730+ for compounded tirzepatide by 31 May 2026. — [Medscape](https://www.medscape.com/viewarticle/fda-proposes-end-mass-glp-1-compounding-2026a1000e06); [Stanford Medicine](https://med.stanford.edu/news/insights/2026/07/glp1s-compounded-why-doctors-worry-about-safety.html); [McDermott](https://www.mcdermottlaw.com/insights/fda-takes-aim-at-misleading-online-claims-for-compounded-glp-1-drugs/); [glp1numbers](https://glp1numbers.com/guides/compounded-glp1-crackdown)
- No reliable count of US compounded GLP-1 users was found.

**Lean mass, protein and training**
- Reviews put lean mass at about 15–40% of total weight lost on GLP-1s. STEP-1 (semaglutide): lean mass about −9.7% vs fat −19.3%. SURMOUNT-1 (tirzepatide): about a quarter of weight lost was lean mass. — [Frontiers 2025 review](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/clinical-diabetes-and-healthcare/articles/10.3389/fcdhc.2025.1720794/full)
- Figures for the same trials vary by secondary source (semaglutide 35–45%, tirzepatide 25–30%; a reported 2026 meta-analysis of 35 RCTs gives a median of 28.3%; unverified). — [remyresearch](https://remyresearch.com/research/glp1-muscle-loss-body-composition); [clinicalnutritionreport](https://www.clinicalnutritionreport.com/articles/preventing-lean-mass-loss-glp1/)
- SEMALEAN (Oct 2025): semaglutide 2.4 mg with preserved lean mass and improved muscle function overall, with weaker effects in people with type 2 diabetes or prior GLP-1 use. — [PMC12673431](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12673431/)
- LEAN-PREP RCT (resistance exercise plus protein during semaglutide/tirzepatide; started Aug 2025, recruiting) has no results yet. — [ClinicalTrials.gov NCT06885736](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06885736)

**Stopping and maintenance**
- Meta-regression of 48 studies: about 60% of weight lost is regained within 1 year of stopping, plateauing at about 75%. — [medRxiv 2025](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.06.09.25328726v1.full); [Cambridge repository](https://www.repository.cam.ac.uk/items/c307d6a7-8d00-4aa9-aa6a-f65a59e178c1)
- Obesity Reviews 2025 (Berg et al.): absolute regain 9.69 kg for semaglutide/tirzepatide vs 2.20 kg for liraglutide; regain proportional to weight lost. — [PubMed 40186344](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40186344/)
- eClinicalMedicine commentary cites real-world data showing up to 65% of users stop GLP-1s within a year. — [Lancet eClinicalMedicine](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/eclinm/article/PIIS2589-5370(26)00240-3/fulltext)

### Inferences
- GLP-1 users are a large, growing and female-skewed (15% vs 9%) group aged 50–64. They overlap heavily with GRIT's food logging and adaptive targets. On drug, appetite is suppressed, so the coach should shift from "eat less" to "hit protein and fibre, drink enough, lift weights". That requires a mode that changes targets (protein floor, e.g. g/kg goal weight; minimum calories so users don't under-eat) and the coach's language.
- Gaps no competitor fully fills, based on the feature lists above:
  1. Linking dose changes and side effects to what was eaten (e.g., nausea after high-fat meals) using GRIT's existing food memory. Shotsy has the dose timeline but weak food data; MFP has food data but a bolted-on medication log.
  2. Lean-mass protection as a measurable programme: strength workouts plus protein streaks plus a "lean mass at risk" warning from weight loss rate, rather than generic advice.
  3. An "off-ramp" maintenance programme when tapering or stopping, with the adaptive calorie engine turning on as appetite returns. Given about 60% regain within a year and up to 65% stopping within a year, this is the largest unmet need and fits GRIT's adaptive targets well.
  4. Social: GLP-1 users are active on Reddit (r/Semaglutide, r/Zepbound) but stigma is real; private, opt-in GLP-1 groups and challenges (protein, steps, lifting) would be distinctive. Medication status must default to private.
- Medication logging and reminders are generally low-risk wellness features; dose recommendations, titration advice or "medication level" estimates edge toward a device or clinical decision support. REG FLAG: Shotsy+ "estimated medication level" chart and any dose guidance. Prescribing (Hims, Ro, WW, Noom Med) is a telehealth business, not an app feature; GRIT could partner rather than prescribe.
- Pills (no injection, daily dosing) change the logging pattern: daily reminders and, for the Wegovy pill, fasting-window timing, rather than weekly injection-site rotation. Apps built around weekly injections may fit poorly.

### Gaps
- No primary-source detail found for Found, Ro Body app features, or r/Semaglutide / r/Zepbound / App Store complaint themes. Sentiment claims about these communities are not evidenced here.
- WeightWatchers' 2026 subscriber numbers, Clinic member count and post-bankruptcy financials not found.
- Number of US compounded GLP-1 users not found; final FDA decision on the 503B exclusion not found.
- Current (Nov 2026) Foundayo and Wegovy pill prices not confirmed.
- Primary-source protein targets for GLP-1 users (e.g., joint society advisories) not retrieved; commonly cited 1.2–1.6 g/kg is unverified here.

## 3. Blood work and biomarkers (Function, Superpower, InsideTracker, Thriva, Medichecks, lab import)

### Takeaway
Consumer "100+ biomarker" memberships have become cheaper and more combative: Function cut its price to $365/yr (Nov 2025), Superpower rose from $199 to $349/yr (Sept 2026), and Function is suing Superpower over how biomarkers are counted. Both are building platforms by buying companies. For GRIT, the opening is not selling tests but importing results the user already has (Apple Health clinical records via FHIR, Android Health Connect Medical Records once stable, or PDF parsing) and turning a few diet-relevant markers into nutrition coaching.

### Cited Findings
- Function Health: about $365/yr (reduced from $499 on 20 Nov 2025 per one source). Advertises 160+ tests over two draws (about 115 markers in the first, about 60 retested in the second). — [bloodtestcomparison (Sept 2026)](https://www.bloodtestcomparison.com/function-vs-superpower); [test-well](https://www.test-well.com/compare/function-health-vs-superpower)
- Function bought Ezra (full-body MRI) in 2025, Getlabs in 2026 and SuppCo (May 2026). It raised $450M more from General Catalyst in July 2026. — [Wikipedia: Function Health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Function_Health)
- Superpower: launched at $199/yr for 100+ biomarkers; $349/yr from 1 Sept 2026 ($399 in NY/NJ). It bought Base (at-home testing) and Feminade (hormonal health). — [merios.life](https://merios.life/compare/function-health-vs-superpower); [healnourishgrow](https://healnourishgrow.com/function-health-vs-superpower/)
- Function sued Superpower in federal court (California) in January 2026 under the Lanham Act. It alleges Superpower's "100+ biomarkers" is misleading because only about 55 are direct lab measurements; the rest are calculated. Superpower calls it baseless; no ruling found. — [FITT Insider](https://insider.fitt.co/function-sues-superpower-over-biomarker-claims/); [SmartCompany](https://www.smartcompany.com.au/startupsmart/superpower-function-health-lawsuit-biomarker-testing/)
- InsideTracker: membership $149/yr, then $340 per Ultimate test (company page; date unclear); about $589 without membership per a third party. — [InsideTracker](https://info.insidetracker.com/subscription); [crowncounseling](https://crowncounseling.com/reviews/insidetracker-review-and-alternative/)
- UK: Thriva finger-prick tests from about £29–59; Medichecks Essential about £89–148, with venous draw add-ons of £25–59 (third-party guides, figures conflict). — [bloodtestguide](https://bloodtestguide.pages.dev/compare/medichecks-vs-thriva/); [lolahealth](https://lolahealth.com/blogs/longevity/cheapest-blood-test-uk)
- WHOOP sells "Advanced Labs" blood testing (no validation details found). — [Medium critique](https://medium.com/@cailbhe/is-whoop-really-able-to-measure-your-healthspan-728b88e69175)

**Importing lab results**
- Apple HealthKit clinical records: read-only access to FHIR resources (including lab results, typically FHIR R4) that the user has downloaded from participating providers into the Health app. It needs the health-records HealthKit entitlement, checked in App Review. Coverage depends on each user's providers. — [Medblocks](https://medblocks.com/blog/how-apple-health-pulls-your-medical-records-the-fhir-stack-explained); [Apple WWDC20 "Handling FHIR without getting burned"](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2020/10669/); [newly.app](https://newly.app/articles/health-records-entitlement)
- Android Health Connect Medical Records: FHIR-based read and write APIs, marked experimental, in Jetpack 1.1.0-beta02, requiring the Android 16 SDK. Google says the Play policy for medical records access is still being written. — [Android developers: Medical Records](https://developer.android.com/health-and-fitness/health-connect/medical-records); [overview](https://developer.android.com/health-and-fitness/eap/phr/overview)
- Expo/React Native: an open GitHub issue on a Capacitor health plugin requests clinical records support, suggesting cross-platform library support is immature. — [GitHub](https://github.com/caplugins/capacitor-health/issues/20)

### Inferences
- Apple Health clinical records only covers US users whose providers support it, so PDF and photo import (LLM extraction of Quest, Labcorp, Function, Thriva or Medichecks reports into structured values) is the more universal route and fits GRIT's existing AI pipeline. Verifying units and reference ranges with the user before use is essential.
- High-value, diet-actionable markers: HbA1c and fasting glucose, lipids (LDL, ApoB, triglycerides, HDL), ferritin and haemoglobin (relevant for menstruating women and GLP-1 users eating less), vitamin D, B12, ALT (fatty liver), TSH, creatinine/eGFR (relevant to high-protein targets). The coach could adjust targets (e.g., fibre and saturated fat for high LDL, iron-rich foods for low ferritin) and track change over time.
- REG FLAG: interpreting individual lab results, flagging abnormal values or recommending treatment can be clinical decision support or a device; storing medical records may bring HIPAA-like duties if GRIT partners with providers, and state consumer health data laws apply regardless. A safer framing is "general nutrition education related to your logged values; discuss with your clinician".
- Complaints about Function/Superpower style services found here centre on counting games (calculated vs measured markers) and price changes; the lawsuit makes "transparent, measured-only" a selling point. Retest prompts (e.g., "it's been 6 months since your lipids") plus showing how logged diet changed alongside lab results would be distinctive, since test companies have little daily behaviour data and GRIT has a lot.

### Gaps
- Mito Health, Quest (questhealth.com) and Labcorp OnDemand pricing and features not researched.
- No independent user-complaint dataset (Trustpilot, App Store) for Function or Superpower found.
- Supported FHIR resource types for Health Connect (lab results specifically) not confirmed.

## 4. Continuous glucose monitors for non-diabetics

### Takeaway
Over-the-counter CGMs (Dexcom Stelo, Abbott Lingo) cost about $50–100/month and both write to Apple Health and Health Connect, so third-party apps can read glucose without a partnership. Evidence that CGMs improve health in non-diabetics is weak: spikes above 180 mg/dL are common in healthy people and experts disagree on what they mean. The CGM coaching market is consolidating around Dexcom (Signos, the only FDA-cleared CGM weight-management system, sells through Stelo.com; Oura integrates Stelo), and Zoe dropped CGMs in its 2025 relaunch.

### Cited Findings
- Stelo: about $89–99/month (two-week single sensor about $55); 15-day wear, 5-minute readings. Lingo: about $84–91/month, single-sensor trial about $49–54; 14-day wear, 1-minute readings. Accuracy (MARD) about 8–10% for both (figures vary). Both sync to Apple Health and Health Connect. Dexcom invested $75M in Oura (Nov 2024) and Oura's Glucose feature (May 2025) shows Stelo data. One source says Stelo data is not available through the Dexcom API as of Oct 2026. Stelo's app has AI photo food logging; Lingo has a "Lingo Score" and coaching. (Affiliate sources, verify prices.) — [sahha.ai](https://sahha.ai/blog/otc-cgm-stelo-vs-lingo/); [bettervitals](https://www.bettervitals.com/learn/dexcom-stelo-vs-abbott-lingo); [endobits](https://endobits.com/stelo-vs-lingo-glp1)
- Signos: FDA cleared on 20 Aug 2025 as the first over-the-counter glucose monitoring system for weight management (Stelo sensor plus AI app). Not intended for medical decisions without a doctor. Company-reported weight loss of up to 7.75% in six months, unpublished. Raised $20M (June 2026) from GV, Dexcom and Blue Cross Blue Shield of Alabama; sold on Stelo.com. — [Fierce Biotech](https://www.fiercebiotech.com/medtech/fda-clears-signos-over-counter-cgm-powered-weight-loss-app); [Drugs.com](https://www.drugs.com/news/fda-clears-otc-glucose-monitoring-weight-management-126380.html); [Femtech Insider](https://femtechinsider.com/signos-raises-20m-for-its-fda-cleared-cgm-based-weight-management-system/)
- Zoe: in Sept 2025 relaunched with the CGM patch and "blue poop" test dropped and the gut microbiome test made optional. Test kit fell from about £299.99 to £149 and membership by about 60%. Earlier it cut about 20% of staff after "overexpanding". 2026 Play reviews complain the redesign made logging harder and removed features. — [The Grocer](https://www.thegrocer.co.uk/news/nutrition-brand-zoe-launches-radically-redesigned-new-app/709452.article); [Raquel Britzke RD](https://raquelbritzke.co.uk/the-zoe-diet-review/); [Welltodo](https://www.welltodoglobal.com/post/zoe-lays-off-staff-amid-restructuring/); [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details/ZOE_Personalized_Nutrition?id=com.joinzoe.results&hl=en_ZA)
- Levels: app membership about $199/yr with sensors optional, works with Dexcom and Libre. Nutrisense: about $149/month with dietitian support. Veri: $39/month app-only, works with Libre 1–3. No shutdowns found. (Third-party sources.) — [bodysciencereview](https://bodysciencereview.pages.dev/blog/levels-cgm-continuous-glucose-monitor-review/); [optimizebiomarkers](https://optimizebiomarkers.pages.dev/cgm-compare/nutrisense-vs-levels); [App Store: Veri](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1496181267)
- Evidence: a meta-analysis of 23 studies (1,074 non-diabetic participants) concluded CGM effectiveness in this group "remains unclear". A 2025 systematic review found CGM "shows promise" for personalising lifestyle changes but could only do a narrative synthesis. — [Springer EJMR 2026](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s40001-026-03920-0); [PMC12612783](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12612783/)
- Over 15% of young healthy adults and more than half of middle-aged and older Framingham participants without diabetes had peaks above 180 mg/dL. In an expert-interpretation study, only 33% of experts recommended follow-up for one ambiguous case, showing a lack of consensus. — [PMC11822776](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11822776/)
- Experts note devices weren't calibrated for non-diabetics and that spike-awareness can cause anxiety. — [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/health/heart-health/glucose-monitoring-blood-sugar-tracking-continuous-non-diabetic-know-rcna189881)
- PROGRESS (Nature Medicine 2025) linked glucose spikes to diet, sleep and activity across normoglycaemia, prediabetes and T2D (observational). — [Nature Medicine](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-025-03849-7)

### Inferences
- GRIT can read blood glucose from HealthKit/Health Connect (written there by Stelo, Lingo and Libre apps) and overlay it on logged meals with no hardware partnership. This gives users a "which of my meals spike me" view inside the food log they already use. The data may arrive with a delay (some CGM apps write to Apple Health in batches; not verified here).
- Framing should follow the evidence: use glucose as a feedback tool for meal composition (fibre, protein, walking after meals), not a scoring system that labels foods "bad" or encourages fear of carbs. The most useful audience is people with prediabetes or GLP-1 users, not healthy young users.
- REG FLAG: Signos needed FDA clearance for a CGM-based weight-management claim. GRIT showing glucose next to meals as wellness is likely lower risk, but claims about diagnosing prediabetes, insulin resistance or treating weight with CGM data would raise device questions.
- Zoe's retreat from CGMs and Levels' move to app-only membership suggest CGM-as-subscription has weak retention; an integrated app that uses occasional two-week CGM "experiments" may fit user behaviour better than continuous wear.

### Gaps
- Whether Stelo and Lingo write every reading or summaries to HealthKit, and with what delay, not confirmed from Apple/Dexcom/Abbott docs.
- Dexcom/Abbott partner API terms for third parties not researched.
- No primary-source OTC CGM market size or Stelo/Lingo user numbers found (Dexcom/Abbott earnings not checked).

## 5. Blood pressure

### Takeaway
Wearable BP is now mainstream but limited: Apple Watch hypertension notifications (FDA-cleared Sept 2025) catch only 41% of hypertension cases, and WHOOP's BP Insights drew an FDA warning letter (July 2025) that was closed after FDA's January 2026 wellness guidance allowed wellness-framed BP. Diet has strong evidence (salt substitutes, DASH), so GRIT's legitimate role is importing readings from cuffs and watches, tracking sodium and potassium in food logs, and coaching DASH-style habits, without diagnosing.

### Cited Findings
- Apple hypertension notifications: FDA-cleared Sept 2025 (Series 11 and other models). Analyses optical heart sensor data over 30-day windows and alerts users to patterns consistent with hypertension. Validation: sensitivity 41%, specificity 92%; better for stage 2 hypertension. A review notes the 510(k) did not need validation against international BP device protocols. — [MobiHealthNews](https://www.mobihealthnews.com/news/apple-receives-fda-nod-hypertension-notification-feature); [Hypertension (AHA)](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/HYPERTENSIONAHA.125.26031); [PMC12826266](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12826266/)
- WHOOP: FDA warning letter 14 Jul 2025 said BP estimates are "inherently associated with the diagnosis of hypo- and hypertension". WHOOP said FDA was "overstepping its authority". FDA later closed the letter for a modified feature, consistent with January 2026 wellness guidance that allows BP for wellness purposes. — [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/07/15/whoop-fda-blood-pressure-feature-wearables.html); [Healthcare Dive](https://www.healthcaredive.com/news/fda-drops-whoop-warning-letter-over-blood-pressure-feature/823737/); [MDDI](https://www.mddionline.com/wearable-medical-devices/fda-backs-down-on-whoop-blood-pressure-monitoring-dispute); [PMC12822547](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12822547/)
- Salt substitutes: a 2022 meta-analysis found −4.6 mmHg systolic and −1.6 mmHg diastolic. SSaSS (20,995 people in rural China, prior stroke or 60+ with high BP) found 14% less stroke, 13% fewer major cardiovascular events and 12% fewer deaths; hyperkalaemia was not significantly increased, though potassium wasn't monitored serially. — [TCTMD](https://www.tctmd.com/news/massive-ssass-study-shows-switch-salt-substitute-cuts-stroke-cvd); [Medicine Today](https://cardiology.medicinetoday.com.au/ct/2022/october/journal-watch/more-support-salt-substitutes-hypertension)
- DASH lowers BP in RCTs and works better combined with low sodium. — [Medicine Today](https://cardiology.medicinetoday.com.au/ct/2022/october/journal-watch/more-support-salt-substitutes-hypertension)

### Inferences
- What GRIT can legitimately do: import BP readings from HealthKit/Health Connect (cuffs such as Omron/Withings write there; Apple's notification is a separate event); track sodium and potassium from food logs (requires reliable micronutrient data in the food database); offer a DASH-style "pattern score" or habit challenges (fruit/veg servings, sodium budget, walking); and show trends in logged readings alongside diet and weight. Weight loss and exercise also lower BP, which GRIT already tracks.
- REG FLAG: estimating BP from sensors, flagging hypertension, or advising on medication is device territory. Potassium-raising advice (salt substitutes) carries risk for people with kidney disease or on certain BP drugs, so it needs a caveat.
- Social challenges (e.g., "sodium under 2,300 mg for 7 days") are a novel, low-risk use of GRIT's social layer; I found no competitor doing this (not systematically checked).

### Gaps
- Hilo (formerly Aktiia) and Samsung Galaxy Watch BP status (FDA clearance, US availability) not found in this research.
- DASH effect size in mmHg not retrieved from a primary source (commonly cited about 11 mmHg systolic in hypertensives in the 1997 DASH trial, unverified here).
- Text of FDA's January 2026 general wellness guidance not retrieved.

## 6. Longevity: Blueprint, Attia-style fitness, healthspan scores

### Takeaway
The best-supported longevity lever an app can track is cardiorespiratory fitness (VO2max), plus strength and muscle. Low fitness is linked to much higher mortality in large observational cohorts. Wearable "biological age" and "healthspan" scores are proprietary, unvalidated composites, and even lab epigenetic clocks are debated. Bryan Johnson's Blueprint shows the commercial fragility of extreme protocols. GRIT can offer evidence-based longevity framing (fitness, strength, protein, sleep, blood pressure, lipids) without the hype.

### Cited Findings
- Mandsager et al. 2018 (JAMA Network Open, 122,007 treadmill-tested patients): risk-adjusted all-cause mortality was lowest in "elite" fitness (≥97.7th percentile) with no upper limit of benefit; low fitness was associated with substantially higher mortality. Fitness was estimated from treadmill METs, not measured VO2max, and the study is observational. — [TCTMD](https://www.tctmd.com/news/big-fitness-equals-big-survival-benefit-new-study-shows); [ACC](https://acc.org/latest-in-cardiology/articles/2019/02/20/08/11/cardiorespiratory-fitness-and-long-term-mortality); [Substack critique](https://nutritionscientist.substack.com/p/does-the-most-famous-vo2-max-study)
- WHOOP Healthspan / "WHOOP Age": a sum of component impacts (sleep, VO2max, etc.), not DNA methylation. Critics note no published formula or confidence intervals and adjustments derived from a healthier, wealthier member population; WHOOP calls it a wellness tool. — [Medium](https://medium.com/@cailbhe/is-whoop-really-able-to-measure-your-healthspan-728b88e69175); [Dalia Health](https://daliahealth.com/blog/how-apps-calculate-biological-age); [Century](https://joincentury.app/blog/2026-07-16-biological-age-wearables)
- Epigenetic clocks: a Nature Aging perspective notes "every new clock defines its own biological age" and that accurate clocks can be built from almost non-overlapping DNA sites. Some clocks (GrimAge) predict mortality and stroke risk better than others. — [npj Aging 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41514-025-00312-2); [PMC12905613](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12905613/)
- Bryan Johnson told WIRED he was "very close to either shutting Blueprint down or selling it" and has been hiring a CEO; reports of $1M/month losses are disputed (Johnson says roughly break-even). Scientists criticise the protocol. — [Fortune (Jul 2025)](https://www.fortune.com/2025/07/24/bryan-johnson-hiring-ceo-blueprint-dont-die-protocol/); [Business Insider](https://www.businessinsider.nl/longevity-enthusiast-bryan-johnson-says-he-doesnt-want-to-run-his-anti-aging-company-anymore)

### Inferences
- Evidence-based longevity features GRIT can build from data it already has: VO2max trend (Apple Watch estimates VO2max and writes it to HealthKit; accuracy not checked here), weekly Zone 2 minutes from heart-rate workouts, strength sessions and progression, protein per kg, steps, sleep regularity, and optionally BP and lipids. A transparent "fitness age" based on published VO2max percentiles is defensible; a proprietary "biological age" is not.
- Social challenges suit longevity behaviours (Zone 2 minutes, step targets, lifting streaks) far better than they suit lab biomarkers, and can be GRIT's distinctive angle compared with solitary dashboards (WHOOP, Oura, Function).
- Avoid supplement stacks, extreme protocols and "reverse your age" claims. REG FLAG: claims that a score predicts disease or lifespan risk may attract FTC scrutiny (advertising substantiation) more than FDA.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed validation of WHOOP Healthspan, Oura or Garmin age scores was found.
- Attia's "Outlive" framework (stability, Zone 2 dosing) not sourced from primary material; Zone 2-specific outcome evidence not researched.
- Market size for longevity consumer products (Grand View etc.) not found from a reliable source.
- Blueprint's status as of Nov 2026 (sold, shut or continuing) not confirmed.
