## 2. Category gaps (ranked)

1. **AI logging is fast but inaccurate, and every app hides it behind a paywall.** In a NUTRITION 2026 study of 102 controlled meals (preliminary), photo features underestimated energy by about 33%: Cal AI by 345 kcal, Lose It! by 333 and MyFitnessPal by 327, with fat undercounted by about 30 g per meal. Accuracy was worse for keto meals. The researchers suggest combining photo AI with traditional database logging. MFP's Meal Scan and Cronometer's photo logging both need a paid tier.
2. **Basic features keep moving behind paywalls, plus ads.** MFP's barcode scan has been Premium-only since 2022, Lose It! is reportedly paywalling barcode scanning for new users, and one market analysis links Lose It!'s falling sentiment to "aggressive paywalling of legacy features". Cal AI was removed from the App Store over its paywall design.
3. **Bad database data.** In an analysis of 50,000 reviews (by a competitor), inaccurate data made up 15.7% of complaints overall and 24.1% among long-term users. MFP's crowdsourced entries draw the most criticism, and Lifesum's barcode scans reportedly double or triple calories.
4. **Targets don't adapt.** On MFP, users set and fix their own targets. MacroFactor's expenditure algorithm is "the whole USP" according to its subreddit, but it costs $72/yr with no free tier, and Carbon has none either.
5. **Logging falls off after a few weeks, and nothing holds people to it.** In two 6-month trials, fewer than half of participants were still self-monitoring by week 10. Supportive-accountability scores correlate with adherence (JMIR 2020). Nutrition apps have weak social features, and Strava and Hevy have strong social features but no nutrition.
6. **AI coaches are siloed and forgetful.** MFP's AI Coach (Jun 2026) covers nutrition only and is Premium-only. Strava's AI summaries cover training only. WHOOP and Oura only recently added memory, and they lack serious food data. Even coaches are wary: 54% worry about the accuracy of AI advice (FitBudd, a vendor survey).
7. **Nutrition and training live in separate apps.** MacroFactor needed a second app and a second subscription for workouts, Fitbod raised prices, and Hevy is free but has no coaching.
8. **Redesigns break habits.** MFP's April 2026 "Today" redesign reportedly drove version ratings from ~3.2 to ~1.5, with no way to revert.

## 3. Recommended Phase 4 moat features (ranked)

### 1. Multimodal "Say / Snap / Type" logging, grounded in a database and your history (BUILD FIRST)
- **What it is.** You type or say "2 eggs, toast with butter, flat white" or snap a photo. The LLM breaks the meal into items, grams and cooking method. Each item is then matched to Open Food Facts, USDA FoodData Central, or your own recent and favourite foods. Before saving, you see a confirm sheet with portion sliders. When something is ambiguous, the app asks one clarifying question, such as "cooked in oil?". Recipe-URL import uses the same pipeline.
- **Why it's a moat for GRIT.** On its own this is now table stakes, but personalised it compounds. Every correction ("my usual oats = 60 g") goes into a per-user food memory, so logging gets faster and more accurate the longer someone uses GRIT, which a new app can't match on day one. Offering it free would hit competitors where they paywall.
- **Evidence of demand.** Lose It! reports 3.5x faster logging and 2x more foods logged with AI (vendor data). MacroFactor, MFP, Cronometer, Lifesum and Yazio all shipped versions in 2025–26, and Cal AI built $30M+/yr of revenue on it.
- **Who does it, and how well.** Accuracy is poor across the board (about −33%). MacroFactor grounds AI Describe in its database, which is the right pattern. Cronometer's version is Gold-only and limited.
- **Build note.** Mobile: expo-camera / expo-image-picker, plus audio sent to Azure OpenAI transcription (or on-device speech). Backend: a NestJS `POST /food/parse` endpoint calls the vision-capable GPT with a strict JSON schema (structured outputs). The LLM estimates portions only; nutrients per gram always come from the database. Prisma models: `ParsedMeal`, `FoodAlias` (user-specific corrections). Reuse the coach's daily cap for cost control, add a "low confidence" flag, and log the photo-vs-confirmed difference to tune prompts.

### 2. Adaptive TDEE with a weekly check-in (BUILD FIRST)
- **What it is.** Estimated expenditure = intake − (change in weight-trend × ~7,700 kcal/kg) / days. Start from the Mifflin estimate and blend toward the observed value as data comes in. Days with incomplete logs are excluded. A weekly check-in proposes a new calorie and macro target, and the coach explains why in one paragraph.
- **Why it's a moat for GRIT.** GRIT already has weigh-ins, intake, MET-based burn, earnback, rollover and a weekly check-in push, so most of the inputs exist. GRIT can do something MacroFactor can't: reconcile logged training with weight trend to auto-calibrate earnback, for example "your workouts burn ~70% of the MET estimate". The estimate improves with personal history, which gives the switching cost MacroFactor users cite.
- **Evidence of demand.** r/MacroFactor users call the expenditure algorithm "the whole USP" and value being told "how much to eat each week". Carbon charges ~$80–120/yr for little more than this.
- **Who does it, and how well.** MacroFactor does it well but behind $72/yr; Carbon is paid only; MFP and Lose It! use static targets.
- **Build note.** A nightly @nestjs/schedule job computes an EWMA weight trend and runs a simple Kalman/Bayesian update. Prisma: `ExpenditureEstimate(date, value, confidence)` and `TargetProposal(status)`. Require at least 14 days of data and about 80% logging completeness. Let the user accept, adjust or ignore each proposal. Add guardrails: a minimum calorie floor and a maximum weekly change. Optionally add cycle-aware smoothing (a known MacroFactor complaint).

### 3. Coach with long-term memory that acts proactively, across nutrition, training, habits and social (BUILD FIRST)
- **What it is.** The coach keeps an editable memory ("What Coach knows about me": goals, injuries, schedule, food dislikes, events), as WHOOP's My Memory does. Event triggers fire proactive messages: two days without logging, protein short three days running, a weight plateau, a PR, a challenge about to be lost, a friend who has been quiet. Through tool calls (with user confirmation), the coach can change the workout plan, habits or targets.
- **Why it's a moat for GRIT.** No competitor holds all four data streams plus a social graph. MFP's coach sees food only, Strava's sees training only, WHOOP's sees recovery without real food data. Memory accumulates per user, so switching means starting over.
- **Evidence of demand.** WHOOP (May 2026) and Oura (2025) shipped memory and proactive check-ins, MFP shipped its AI Coach (Jun 2026), and MFP built a ChatGPT integration. On the other side, Future dropped its AI coach in favour of human coaches, a sign that generic AI coaches disappoint.
- **Build note.** Prisma model: `CoachMemory(category, text, source, confidence, expiresAt)`. Retrieve memories with pgvector on Supabase, or with a simple category filter at first. After each chat, the GPT extracts memory candidates through a tool call. A rules engine (cron) detects triggers, the GPT drafts the message, and it is delivered through the existing inbox and push. Register tools such as `updateWorkoutPlan`, `proposeTarget` and `addHabit`, and require user approval for each change. Add a privacy screen to view and delete memories.

### 4. Squads: small accountability groups and partners
- **What it is.** Groups of 2–6 people with shared weekly commitments (log five days, hit protein, three workouts). Challenges can be built on habits and nutrition, not just steps and minutes. Partners can do a "check on a friend" one-tap nudge, and the coach writes a weekly squad recap.
- **Why it's a moat for GRIT.** This is a social-graph network effect: the more friends someone has on GRIT, the harder it is to leave. It reuses GRIT's existing friends, kudos, challenges and streak-at-risk push. MacroFactor and Cronometer have essentially no social features, and Strava and Hevy have no nutrition.
- **Evidence of demand.** Self-monitoring collapses by week 10, and supportive accountability correlates with adherence (JMIR 2020). NCT05180448 is testing data-sharing with groups and friends, but its results are unpublished, so this is unverified.
- **Build note.** Prisma: `Squad`, `SquadMember`, `SquadGoal`, `Nudge`. Generalise the challenge engine's metric source to habits and nutrition. Rate-limit nudges. This is mostly CRUD plus push, a low-risk build.

### 5. GLP-1 mode
- **What it is.** Medication and dose-day log, side-effect journal, protein-first and lower-volume targets, hydration and fibre habits, and a strength-training emphasis to preserve muscle (GRIT's training data is an edge here). Also a "coming off the drug" maintenance plan.
- **Why it's a moat for GRIT.** It combines nutrition, training, habits and coach where competitors offer nutrition-only logging. The muscle-preservation and maintenance angles are underserved.
- **Evidence of demand.** 12% of US adults are currently taking a GLP-1 (KFF, Nov 2025). MFP made its GLP-1 support free (Apr 2026), MyNetDiary launched a companion (May 2026), and Noom built a telehealth business around the drugs.
- **Caveat.** This is quickly becoming table stakes and is not deeply defensible. Avoid dosing advice; it is a medical and regulatory risk.
- **Build note.** Add a med-log model, reuse habits for reminders, add a target preset, and give the coach prompt context and guardrails.

### 6. Progressive-overload strength tracking linked to nutrition
- **What it is.** Set, rep and load logging, PRs and e1RM, auto-progression suggestions inside the coach's weekly plan. Training-day versus rest-day macros feed into feature #2.
- **Why it's a moat for GRIT.** It joins what MacroFactor sells as two apps and two subscriptions. Fitbod's price hike and exercise-rotation complaints leave an opening.
- **Caveat.** This is the largest build and Hevy is cheap and good, so treat it as later Phase 4. Sleep- and recovery-aware coaching should ride on the Health Connect / Apple Health work already in progress, as an input to #3 rather than a separate feature.
- **Build note.** Prisma models: `Exercise`, `WorkoutSet`. Use an exercise library seed (e.g. the open-source free-exercise-db). Drive progression with rules such as double progression or RPE, and let the coach explain them.

**Deprioritised.** Meal planning: MFP Premium+ owns it and LLM meal plans are generic, so it's better offered as a coach action. Restaurant logging: data licensing is hard, so fold it into #1 ("Chipotle bowl, chicken, rice…").

**Build first: #1, #2, #3.** Free AI logging removes the category's biggest pain and paywall. Adaptive targets create personal-data lock-in. A cross-domain memory coach is the one thing no competitor can copy without owning all four data types. #4 is cheap and can ship alongside.

**Could not verify:** Lose It!, Cronometer, Lifesum, Carbon and Ladder prices; whether Yazio has photo logging; WHOOP/Oura subscription prices; the MacroFactor bundle price; Future cancelling its AI coach (one source); Lose It!'s GLP-1 feature. Treat Reddit sentiment as indicative: no 2026 r/MacroFactor threads were readable.
